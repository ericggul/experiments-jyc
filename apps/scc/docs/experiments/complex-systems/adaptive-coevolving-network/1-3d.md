# `/adaptive-coevolving-network/1-3d` — coevolving voters in a volume (2026-10-05)

- **Question:** can route 1's loop `opinion → which ties survive → layout → who can influence whom → opinion` be read in a turning 3D volume, in the dark particle-field register of [`/attractor/3`](../../dynamical-systems/attractor/README.md)?
- **Baseline:** [`/adaptive-coevolving-network/1`](README.md#route-1-the-closed-loop). The model is a verbatim copy of route 1's `model.ts` (its tests are copied too); defaults (500 voters, cap 800, φ = .5, drift .0015, 2 updates per voter per second) are unchanged.
- **Changed variable:** geometry and rendering only. The force law is route 1's (repulsion, springs at .62 ideal length, gravity .9) with a z axis. The layout is computed in units of one ideal tie length; the view follows the cloud's centroid and RMS radius (smoothed at rate 1.2/s) so islands drifting apart read as the volume opening, not as a zoom jump.
- **Encoding (revised 2026-10-05 after a side-by-side browser comparison with `/attractor/3`):** the first version, with large glowing opinion-coloured blobs, 1 px lines and rings, was rejected as tacky. Now everything is the reference's sprite: a tiny additive disc with a linear falloff, on black, coloured by the reference's gradient (warm `#f7b373` at the cloud's centre to blue `#3d6ef5` at about 1.3 RMS radii). Opinion only tints that gradient (50%). Voters are motes of .065–.113 world units, larger with degree; every tie is a stream of 2–7 dust particles (.01–.04) flowing between its ends. Agreeing ties drift slowly; disagreeing ties run white-hot and three to four times faster. A rewired tie streams out from its voter over .35 s; adoption, drift, planting and arrivals brighten the voter for .9 s. The volume turns at .03 rad/s; reduced motion stops the turn and flashes and slows the model to .3, as route 1 does. Camera and controls follow the reference: default fov 75 at (−4, 3, 4), orbit without pan; the slider and hint use the reference's mono labels.
- **Interaction:** drag empty space to orbit (no pan, distance 2.2–11). Tap adds a voter with the least-held opinion, on the pointer ray at the depth of the voter nearest on screen, tied to its two nearest voters in 3D. Dragging that starts on a voter (within 16 px) paints that opinion in a 1.1 ideal-length sphere around the voter under the pointer. N and Enter act at the cloud's centre. The adopt–rewire slider is route 1's.
- **Renderer and budget:** R3F `Canvas` with the default WebGL renderer and one GLSL point `ShaderMaterial` in two draw calls (tie dust, voters), not `/attractor/3`'s WebGPU compute: the state is a CPU graph model, so there is nothing to compute on the GPU. The total sprite count is capped at 8,192 (800 voters plus up to 7,392 tie particles). Autonomous frames run at 24 Hz (`frameloop="demand"`); orbiting renders on demand. At the user's request (sharper image), DPR is `[1, 2]` and point sizes are scaled by DPR. On the user's Mac this was observed in Chrome on 2026-10-05: it rendered, with no WebGL errors, only the non-fatal `THREE.Clock` deprecation warning. Buffers are preallocated; each frame uploads only the used range, and the force scratch arrays are reused.
- **Measured, Node only:** layout 0.6 ms per frame on average at 500 voters, 1.3 ms at 800 (single spikes up to about 27 ms, probably JIT warm-up). At φ = .5 the graph had split into 3–6 components by 30–50 s; at 800 voters and φ = .8 it had about 10 components by 20 s, and the RMS radius doubled.
- **Unresolved:** whether islands separate clearly enough in depth without route 1's flat overview; whether 50% opinion tint keeps six opinions distinguishable once islands form.
