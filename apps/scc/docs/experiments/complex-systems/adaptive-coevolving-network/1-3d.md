# `/adaptive-coevolving-network/1-3d` — coevolving voters in a volume (2026-10-05)

- **Question:** can route 1's loop `opinion → which ties survive → layout → who can influence whom → opinion` be read in a turning 3D volume, in the dark particle-field register of [`/attractor/3`](../../dynamical-systems/attractor/README.md)?
- **Baseline:** [`/adaptive-coevolving-network/1`](README.md#route-1-the-closed-loop). The model is a verbatim copy of route 1's `model.ts` (its tests are copied too); defaults (500 voters, cap 800, φ = .5, drift .0015, 2 updates per voter per second) are unchanged.
- **Changed variable:** geometry and rendering only. The force law is route 1's (repulsion, springs at .62 ideal length, gravity .9) with a z axis. The layout is computed in units of one ideal tie length; the view follows the cloud's centroid and RMS radius (smoothed at rate 1.2/s) so islands drifting apart read as the volume opening, not as a zoom jump.
- **Encoding:** the first version (large glowing blobs, 1 px lines, rings) was rejected as tacky after a side-by-side browser comparison with `/attractor/3`. Everything now uses the reference's sprite: a tiny additive disc with linear falloff on black, over the reference's gradient (warm `#f7b373` at the cloud's centre, blue `#3d6ef5` toward its edge). Opinion is the main colour (85% over the gradient; orange, blue, aqua, rose, violet, citron), so islands of agreement read as separate colours. Voters are motes of .065–.113 world units, larger with degree. Ties are screen-facing quads of constant pixel width (.8 px agreeing, .9 px disagreeing), drawn very dim (gain .10/.14), because additive ties saturate the centre to white: at 1.1–1.6 px and gain .42/.62 the whole structure was lost, and dust particles alone (2–7 per tie) did not read as ties. Each tie also carries a dim stream of 2–7 dust particles; disagreements flow faster. A rewired tie grows out from its voter over .35 s; opinion changes brighten the voter for .9 s.
- **Interaction:** every drag orbits (damping .08, no pan, distance 1.5–14). The first version painted when a drag started within 16 px of a voter; at this density almost every press did, so orbiting failed, and painting by drag was removed. A tap that stays within 6 px either plants the least-held opinion in a 1.1 ideal-length sphere around the voter under it (within 10 px), or adds a voter with that opinion on the pointer ray at the depth of the nearest voter, tied to its two nearest voters in 3D. N and Enter act at the cloud's centre. The adopt–rewire slider is route 1's.
- **Renderer and budget:** R3F `Canvas` with the default WebGL renderer and GLSL `ShaderMaterial`s in three draw calls (instanced tie quads, tie dust, voters), not `/attractor/3`'s WebGPU compute: the state is a CPU graph model, so there is nothing to compute on the GPU. The total sprite count is capped at 8,192 (800 voters plus up to 7,392 tie particles). Autonomous frames run at 24 Hz (`frameloop="demand"`); orbiting renders on demand. Model and layout follow real time: a frame gap up to .25 s is caught up in at most 6 layout substeps. Before this, a hidden or throttled tab ran the model at a small fraction of real time. At the user's request (sharper image), DPR is `[1, 2]` and point sizes are scaled by DPR. On the user's Mac this was observed in Chrome on 2026-10-05: it rendered, with no WebGL errors, only the non-fatal `THREE.Clock` deprecation warning. Buffers are preallocated; each frame uploads only the used range, and the force scratch arrays are reused.
- **Measured, Node only:** layout 0.6 ms per frame on average at 500 voters, 1.3 ms at 800 (single spikes up to about 27 ms, probably JIT warm-up). At φ = .5 the graph had split into 3–6 components by 30–50 s; at 800 voters and φ = .8 it had about 10 components by 20 s, and the RMS radius doubled.
- **Offline projection check (Node, same model, layout, colours and camera, φ = .5):** at 45 s six coloured islands are distinct but still linked; at 90 s they are separate. The first 10–20 s are a random-graph hairball by construction.
- **Unresolved:** whether islands separate clearly enough in depth without route 1's flat overview; whether 50% opinion tint keeps six opinions distinguishable once islands form.
