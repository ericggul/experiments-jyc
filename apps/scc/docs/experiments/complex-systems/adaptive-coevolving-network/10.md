# Route 10: Axelrod culture on a coevolving network

Route: `/adaptive-coevolving-network/10` (2026-10-02). Component: `components/complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/10/`.

## Model card

- **Object and boundary:** a deliberately synthetic abstraction. It is not an empirical claim about any society. A fixed population of 120 agents, each holding a culture of F = 3 features with q traits each, sits on a simple undirected graph with 360 ties (mean degree 6). The number of ties is conserved. Positions are not part of the model.
- **Update (one elementary step):** pick a random agent i and one of its ties to j. Let o = (features shared by i and j) / F.
  - o = 1: the tie is inert.
  - 0 < o < 1: with probability o, i copies one feature where it differs from j, chosen uniformly (Axelrod 1997).
  - o = 0: i drops the tie and links to a random agent it is not already tied to. The tie leaves j and stays with i. This is homophilous rewiring with threshold 0, as in Centola et al. 2007 and Vazquez et al. 2007.
- **Drift:** with probability r per elementary update, a random agent sets one random feature to a random trait (Centola et al. 2007).
- **Time:** one model time unit is one update per agent (120 updates). The screen runs at 6 units per second, or 0.3 times that under reduced motion.
- **Parameters:**
  - q ∈ [2, 40] (integer, default 20). Changing it redraws every culture uniformly from q traits and keeps the ties: "a new generation of cultures on the current network".
  - r ∈ [0, .02] (default .0005).
  - N = 120, ⟨k⟩ = 6, F = 3, seed `0x3c6ef372`.
- **Macro observables:** number of distinct cultures, share of the largest culture, number of connected components, share of the largest component, and the share of active (partial-overlap) and stranger (zero-overlap) ties.
- **Loop:** `culture → which ties have zero overlap → rewiring → who can influence whom → culture`.

## Screen encoding

- Each agent is a disc cut into three sectors, one per feature, in a fixed order with the first at the top. Each sector takes its trait's colour from one palette shared by all features: golden-angle hues with three lightness steps, so adjacent trait numbers never look alike. Identical cultures are identical glyphs. Disc radius is 4–8 px, following the ideal tie length.
- Ties are ink, and their opacity follows overlap: about .1 for one shared feature, .22 for two, .42 and slightly thicker for three. Zero-overlap ties are dashed at .42. They are the only ties that can be cut, so a dashed tie means "about to be rewired".
- Event marks (.7 s, capped at 240):
  - A copy flashes the tie from source to copier in the copied trait's colour.
  - A rewire leaves a dashed trace to the abandoned neighbour while the new tie draws out.
  - Drift and participant changes ring the agent.
- Reduced motion turns event marks off.
- White field. No title, legend or counters. The macro observables reach screen readers only, every 2 s.
- **Views** (layout and views never feed back into the model):
  - *네트워크:* browser force layout of current ties, copied from route 2. Islands separate because nothing ties them together.
  - *문화:* one disc per distinct culture, with area proportional to its members. Discs spiral out by cumulative size, so the largest culture is centred. Ties crossing between discs are exactly the ties that can still copy or rewire. After fragmentation, none cross.
  - *닮음:* classical MDS of the cultural distance 1 − o. It uses power iteration warm-started from the previous axes so the map does not flip, and it is recomputed every .5 s while shown. Members of one culture share a small sunflower disc. Label: "가까울수록 문화가 닮음".
  - View switches use route 2's grammar: a .9 s cubic in-out from the snapshot to the live targets, then exponential following at 12/s.

## Interaction

- Tap an agent to give it a random culture (a stranger), usually creating dashed ties that are then cut.
- Drag from an agent to carry its whole culture. Every agent the pointer passes within about 1.6 glyph radii + 8 px takes it. A ghost glyph follows the pointer.
- Keyboard on the focused canvas: Enter gives a random culture to the agent nearest the centre. Space imposes that agent's culture on all its neighbours.
- The bottom 옵션/닫기 toggle opens:
  - a three-sentence Korean model description;
  - the views 네트워크 · 문화 · 닮음;
  - the q slider 획일–다양;
  - the drift slider 고정–표류.
- The hint "누르면 낯선 문화, 끌면 그 문화를 옮김" shows until the first interaction.

- **Few traits:** q ≤ 3 gives one global culture in one connected network within about 30 s.
- **Many traits:** q ≥ 16 fragments the network within 30–45 s into monocultural islands. With r = 0 the end state is frozen (no active or stranger ties), with exactly as many cultures as components (seed 7919, q = 24, 400 units).
- **Drift:** this compares drift rates at 250 units, 6–8 seeds.
  - With q = 24, r = 0 gives about 8.4 parts with a largest part of .39. r = .01 gives 3.2 parts and .81. r = .02 gives 1.8 parts and .99, with about 34 cultures.
  - Strong drift therefore keeps a culturally disordered population connected.
  - Drift up to .004 applied to already-frozen islands does not reconnect them, because a mutant shares two features with its island and is reabsorbed.
- **Redrawing:** after fragmentation at q = 24 (250 units, seeds 7919, 15838, 23757), redrawing with q = 2 brought the largest part to at least .98 on all three seeds within 50–100 units. At 300 units, the largest culture was 1.00, .98 and .66. The third seed was still settling between two cultures.
- **Event rate:** at defaults, about 230 events per screen second at the start, falling to near zero once islands freeze.

## Cost

Model, layout and view targets together averaged .12 ms per frame in Node, with a p99 of 1.5–1.7 ms, at N = 120 for q = 20 and q = 40 with r = .02. The p99 comes from the similarity embedding, about 1.5 ms every .5 s; its first call takes about 18 ms while the JIT warms up. N = 400 would be .9 ms on average with a 70 ms embedding, so the population is fixed at 120. This has not been profiled in a browser.

## Tests

- `model.test.ts`:
  - deterministic replay;
  - copies happen only across partial overlap and add one shared feature;
  - rewires happen only across zero overlap;
  - tie count is conserved and the graph stays simple;
  - q = 2 gives one culture (> .8) in one network (> .95);
  - q = 24 gives more than 5 parts and a largest part under .6, freezing with one culture per component;
  - r = .02 keeps the largest part above .9 with fewer than half the parts of r = 0;
  - redrawing with q = 2 reconnects frozen islands;
  - intervention bounds.
- `views.test.ts`:
  - every view stays inside wide and phone frames, both at the start and after convergence;
  - the 문화 view separates cultures;
  - the MDS map orders a shares-two pair closer than a shares-none pair;
  - the map is stable when the state does not change.

## Unresolved

- With only 120 agents, the q_c of the coevolving model is not sharp, and the recombination transition of Vazquez et al. (very large q) does not appear within q ≤ 40, F = 3.
- Changing q redraws all cultures. This is a restart rather than a gradual change in the trait space. Whether participants read it as such is untested.
- At large q, the palette's 40 hues are not all distinguishable. Equal traits are exactly equal colours, but two near hues can look alike. Tie opacity carries the exact overlap.
- Copy flashes at about 200 events/s at the start may read as noise until domains form.
