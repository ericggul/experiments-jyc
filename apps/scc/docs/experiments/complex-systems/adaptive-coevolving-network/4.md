# Adaptive coevolving network / 4: Physarum transport network

Route: `/adaptive-coevolving-network/4`. Built 2026-10-02. Component: `components/complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/4/`.

## Model card

- **Object and boundary.** A deliberately synthetic abstraction of a *Physarum polycephalum* plasmodium: a flat sheet of tubes between food sources. Flow inside the tubes and tube thickness are the only dynamics. There is no growth front, chemotaxis, oscillation or nutrient budget. This is a mathematical demonstrator of the Tero flow–conductance rule, not a calibrated biological simulation.
- **Sheet.** A jittered triangular mesh (jitter ±.14 spacing, rim junctions unjittered) with about 200–520 junctions, scaled to the first screen's aspect ratio (`AREA_PER_JUNCTION = 1700` px², capped at `MAX_JUNCTIONS = 520`). Tube length `Lᵢⱼ` is the Euclidean distance in mesh spacings. Geometry is part of the model; the browser only scales it to fit.
- **Node state:** pressure `pᵢ`. **Edge state:** conductance `Dᵢⱼ ∈ [0, 1]` (0 while cut), the latest flux `Qᵢⱼ`, and `drive`, the growth signal `f(|Q|)` smoothed over about one time unit.
- **Each step** (`Δt = .05`): one food is drawn uniformly at random as the source. It injects `I₀`, and every other food withdraws `I₀/(F − 1)` (Tero et al. 2010). Pressures solve Kirchhoff's law
  `Σⱼ Dᵢⱼ/Lᵢⱼ (pᵢ − pⱼ) + ε pᵢ = Iᵢ`, with `ε = 10⁻⁵`.
  The weak leak to ground `ε` keeps the system solvable when cuts isolate food. The solver is Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradient, warm-started from the last pressures, with relative tolerance `10⁻⁴` and at most 400 iterations. Then
  `Qᵢⱼ = Dᵢⱼ/Lᵢⱼ (pᵢ − pⱼ)`,
  `dDᵢⱼ/dt = f(|Qᵢⱼ|) − γ Dᵢⱼ`, with `f(Q) = Q^μ / (1 + Q^μ)`,
  integrated by explicit Euler. Intact tubes keep a floor of `D ≥ .002`, so a starved region can be recolonised.
- **Loop:** conductance → pressures → flux → conductance. A thick tube draws more flux, which keeps it thick; its neighbours lose flux and thin.
- **Cuts:** a cut sets `D = 0` and removes the tube from the system. After `HEAL_TIME = 40` units it grows back at the floor and must then win flux like any other tube.
- **Parameters and defaults:** `μ = 1.1` (slider range `[.75, 1.5]`), `γ = 1`, `I₀ = 1`. Four starting foods sit at fractions (.15, .25), (.8, .2), (.5, .8) and (.2, .75) of the sheet, read transposed on a tall sheet. At most 10 foods. Every tube starts at `D = 1`, a uniform plasmodium.
- **Why no γ or I₀ slider:** under Kirchhoff, scaling every `D` by `c` scales `p` by `1/c` and leaves `Q` unchanged. With `D' = γD`, the rule becomes `dD'/dt = γ (f − D')`, so γ only rescales thickness and tempo. `I₀` does change topology through the saturation of `f`, but it pushes the same redundancy/efficiency trade-off as μ. A second slider would have duplicated the first.
- **Macro observables** (`measureMould`). Living tubes are those with `D ≥ .05`. The record covers their count and summed length, independent loops (edges − nodes + components), whether all food is joined, and the fragile share: living tubes whose loss alone would split the food.

## Screen encoding

White field. Ink is tube wall; amber `rgb(226,150,0)` is protoplasm. Food and the flow it drives share the amber.

- Every intact tube is a 7% ink hairline, so cuts read as gaps in the sheet until they heal.
- The tube wall is ink with width `0.6 + 0.2·s·√D` px, where `s` is pixels per mesh spacing. Opacity fades below about `2·LIVING`. Widths are batched into 14 levels.
- The amber core inside each tube has width `0.55 · 0.2·s·√(drive/γ)`: the thickness the flow is asking for, drawn at 55%.
  - A settled tube shows an ink rim around an amber core.
  - A tube still thickening shows amber wider than its wall.
  - A starving tube is bare ink that thins away.

  This is the coevolution loop drawn without text: flow leads, wall follows.
- Food is a filled amber disc of radius `max(5, .17·s)`.
- Event marks (.8 s, off under reduced motion):
  - a cut leaves the two severed ends of the tube, at its former width, retracting from the cut;
  - placed food rings outward and removed food rings inward.
- There is no title, legend or counter, and no views. A flux/conductance view split was considered; the core-in-wall overlay already shows both quantities on the same mark.

## Interaction

- Tapping bare sheet places food at the nearest junction. Tapping food (within `max(18 px, .5 spacing)`) removes it. Dragging food carries it junction to junction. Dragging across bare sheet (more than 6 px) cuts every intact tube the stroke crosses.
- Keyboard on the focused canvas:
  - Enter or Space places food at the junction farthest from all food;
  - Delete or Backspace removes the newest food;
  - X cuts a 3.2-spacing stroke square across the thickest tube that does not touch food.
- A bottom 옵션/닫기 toggle, as in route 2, opens a short Korean description of the model and one slider, 여러 갈래 ↔ 한 길 (μ). A one-line hint, 누르면 먹이를 놓고, 그으면 관을 자릅니다, shows until the first interaction.
- The canvas has `touch-action: none` and pointer capture. An sr-only summary every 2 s gives the food count, living tubes, loops and fragile share.
- Tempo: 1.5 model units per second, 30 solves per second, at most 3 solves per frame. Reduced motion slows this to 0.3× and turns off the marks.

## Measured pure-model results (Node, seed `0x5f3759df`, 2026-10-02)

- **Contraction:** from the uniform sheet (1013 tubes at 364 junctions, aspect 1.6), the living set falls to about 50–80 tubes within 20 units (about 13 s on screen). It briefly fragments below the threshold at about t = 5–10 while paths are chosen.
- **μ sweep**, 364 junctions, 4 foods, t = 80–160 (three seeds agree within noise):

  | μ | living tubes | loops | fragile share |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | .7 | ≈ 325 | ≈ 93 | 0, an almost undissolved sheet |
  | .8 | ≈ 190–220 | ≈ 30–35 | 0 |
  | .9 | ≈ 110–120 | ≈ 7–10 | ≤ .08 |
  | 1.0 | ≈ 80–90 | ≈ 5–7 | ≤ .16 |
  | 1.1 (default) | ≈ 52–76 | ≈ 5 | ≈ 0 |
  | 1.2 | ≈ 28–40 | 1–2 | .25–.8 |
  | ≥ 1.3 | 26 | 0 | 1, a Steiner-like tree where every tube is a single point of failure |

  On the phone sheet (198 junctions, aspect .51), μ = .75 gives 177 tubes and 62 loops, μ = 1.1 gives 48 tubes and 5 loops, and μ ≥ 1.3 gives 26 tubes, 1 loop and fragile share .23. On the desktop sheet (504 junctions, aspect 2.15), μ = .75 gives 347 tubes and 58 loops, μ = 1.1 gives 85 tubes and 9 loops, and μ = 1.5 gives 32 tubes, 0 loops and fragile share 1.
- **Cut and reroute** (μ = 1.1, settled 60 units, a 3.2-spacing stroke across the thickest tube, 7 tubes cut): food stays joined; the living set grows from 53 to about 75 tubes within 1 unit as detour tubes thicken, then relaxes to about 70. At μ = 1.4 (tree) the food is split for 2–4 units before a detour reconnects it. At μ = .8 the parallel tubes absorb the cut at once.
- **New food** at (.85, .7): it joins within 2–4 units at μ ≤ 1.1 and about 8 units at μ = 1.4. Total length rises from 52 to about 75 at μ = 1.1.
- **Removing food** down to one: every tube falls below the living threshold within 5 units.

## Tests

`model.test.ts` (`node --test`, 10 tests):

- the mesh is simple, triangular and consistent;
- seeded replay is deterministic;
- Kirchhoff holds: net flux at non-food junctions stays under `2·10⁻³`, and the source emits `I₀`;
- the uniform sheet contracts below 12% of tubes while food stays joined;
- **causal contrast in μ:** at μ = .8, 15 or more loops and fragile share under .05; at μ = 1.4, 0 loops, fragile share over .95, and more than 4× shorter;
- **cut contrast:** the same seed with and without a cut across the busiest tube. After 8 units the food is still joined and at least 3 detour tubes are living that the uncut twin left thin;
- cut tubes heal at `HEAL_TIME`;
- new food is reached and total length grows;
- with fewer than two foods everything withers;
- food bounds: 10 at most, no duplicates, no move onto other food.

## Unresolved

- Under the random-source rule each step's flux flickers between sources. The drawn core uses the smoothed `drive`, which is honest about what grows tubes but hides the instantaneous direction. Flow direction is not shown.
- Paths follow the triangular lattice directions, so "shortest" routes zigzag. A Delaunay mesh of random points would look more organic but would make tube lengths less uniform.
- The living threshold `.05` is a measurement convention. Near μ ≈ .9 on large sheets, food can stay joined only through sub-threshold tubes, and `connected` then reads false.
- Healing after 40 units is a design choice to keep the sheet usable without a reset. The source papers do not model cuts.
- Browser cost and the legibility of the core-versus-wall encoding on a phone have not been checked.
