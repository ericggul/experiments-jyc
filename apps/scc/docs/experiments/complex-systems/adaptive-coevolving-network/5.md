# Adaptive coevolving network / 5: structural balance

Route: `/adaptive-coevolving-network/5`. Component: `components/complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/5/` (`StructuralBalanceFive`). Date: 2026-10-02.

## Model card

- **Object and boundary.** A synthetic mathematical demonstrator: a complete signed network of 30 people (cap 48). Every pair is friends (+1) or enemies (−1). It makes no empirical claim about any real group.
- **Coevolution loop.** `signs → which triangles are unbalanced → which relationship changes next → signs`. A person's state is their row of signs. Every change rebalances one triangle and flips the balance of each of the other `n − 2` triangles that share the pair.
- **Balance.** A triangle is balanced when `s_ij s_ik s_jk = +1`. The count of unbalanced triangles `U` is maintained exactly, and each flip changes it by `Σ_k s_ij s_ik s_jk` taken before the flip.
- **Resolution** (continuous time, Gillespie). Each unbalanced triangle fires at rate `pressure = .1`, and the total rate is capped at `maxRate = 40`. The cap only stretches the busiest moments. The sequence of changes stays the same.
  - `(+,+,−)`: with probability `p` (the reconcile slider), the two enemies make up. This is Antal, Krapivsky and Redner's local triad dynamics (LTD). Otherwise the person in the middle takes a side: of their two friendships, the one whose loss leaves fewer unbalanced triangles in the whole network becomes enmity. Ties are broken at random. This is the constraint from Antal's constrained triad dynamics (CTD).
  - `(−,−,−)`: the pair whose alliance leaves the fewest unbalanced triangles becomes friends.
- **Unrest.** Independently, a random pair flips at total rate `u` (slider, `[0, 1]`, default `.2`), so a balanced state keeps being tested.
- **Defaults.** `p = .3`, `u = .2`, random initial signs with friendly probability `.5`, seed `0x5bd1e995`.
- **Why this hybrid and not pure LTD.** Pure LTD on a complete graph at `p < ½` was prototyped at N = 20 and 30. It did not balance within 200 000 updates and stayed in a disordered quasi-stationary state (friendly density about .35–.6, no camp structure). Antal et al. predict this: the time to reach balance grows exponentially with N. The participant would never see two camps form. Adding the CTD constraint to the hostile moves keeps LTD's `p` and its ½ threshold, and makes `p < ½` settle into two camps.
- **Macro observable.** Whether the network is balanced, and the size of the smaller camp. Camps come from the sign of the leading eigenvector of the sign matrix, which is exact for a balanced network. By Harary's theorem, a balanced complete graph has at most two camps.

## Screen encoding

- White field. People are ink dots of fixed size, since every person has degree `n − 1`.
- Friendship is an ink line and enmity a red line (`214, 58, 34`). Lines are faint by default. A relationship that sits in unbalanced triangles is drawn stronger in four steps of its tension, which is the share of its triangles that are unbalanced. Those are the only places where the next change can happen, so a disordered start reads as a dark tangle that clears as balance arrives.
- Each change flashes the unbalanced triangle that forced it as a thin ink outline, and draws the changed relationship heavy in its new sign. Both fade over .8 s. Unrest flips and participant flips get only the heavy line. Rings mark an outcast (red) and a newcomer (ink).
- There is no title, legend or counter. The sr-only summary updates every 2 s with balance, camp sizes and the friendly share.
- **Layout** (browser only, never read by the model). Each person moves toward the average of springs to their friends (rest length .45 of the ideal length) and the average of constant-strength pushes from their enemies. They also feel personal-space repulsion and gravity corrected for aspect ratio. Balanced states draw as one compact cluster (peace) or two clusters facing each other across the long axis (camps). A person who changes camp crosses the field.
- **Views.** Each view maps people to target points. A switch eases every point over .9 s (cubic in-out) from a snapshot to the live targets, then points follow at rate 12/s. Lines and matrix cells cross-fade.
  - *관계망:* the force layout, the default.
  - *원:* a circle ordered by camp, then by id. Camp +1 is on the left arc and camp −1 on the right, with an empty slot between them. In balance, red chords only cross between the arcs.
  - *행렬:* people sit on the diagonal ordered by camp, and relationship (i, j) is a cell at (x of j, y of i). Balance is two ink diagonal blocks with red off-diagonal blocks. The cells move with the people when camps reorder.

## Interaction

- Drag from one person to another to flip their relationship. While dragging, the line previews the sign it will take.
- Tap a person to make them everyone's enemy.
- Tap empty space to add a newcomer who is everyone's friend (cap 48).
- Keys on the focused canvas: Enter or Space isolates the person nearest the centre; F flips the relationship between that person and the person farthest from them on screen; N adds a newcomer at the centre.
- A bottom 옵션/닫기 toggle in route 2's grammar opens:
  - a four-sentence Korean model description;
  - views 관계망 / 원 / 행렬;
  - a 편 가르기–화해 slider (`p`);

## Measured pure-model results (N = 30, 2026-10-02)

Random starts used seeds `7919·s`, `s = 1…4`. Times are in model units, and one unit is one second on screen.

| Condition | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| random start, `p = 0` | balanced at 13–18 s; two camps, minority 14–15 |
| random start, `p = .3` | balanced at 13–16 s; minority 14 |
| random start, `p = .45` | balanced at 24–36 s; minority 8–13 |
| random start, `p = .55` | balanced at 30–61 s; universal friendship |
| random start, `p = .7` | balanced at 14–19 s; universal friendship |
| settled camps, raise to `p = .7`, `u = .2` | a peace cascade (`U` jumps to 500–1400, then 0) reaches universal friendship within 15–90 s in 4/4 runs |
| settled camps, raise to `p = .7`, `u = 0` | unchanged: balance is absorbing |
| peace, lower to `p = .3`, `u = .2` | people are expelled one at a time; minority 4–8 after 180 s |
| camps, `p = .3`, isolate one person | rebalanced after 16–19 changes (≈ 1–3 s); the outcast rejoins a camp |
| peace, isolate one person | already balanced; a camp of one that stays |
| camps, one cross-camp reconciliation | at `p = .7`, universal friendship in 3/4 runs (≈ 15 s, ≈ 500 changes); at `p = .3`, healed after 1–9 changes |

Pure LTD for comparison (N = 30, 200 000 updates): at `p ≤ .45` it never balanced, and at `p ≥ .55` it reached peace after 1300–2400 updates.

## Cost

Model step, tension, factions, layout and view targets were measured over 3600 frames in Node:

- N = 30, `u = .2`: .04 ms average, 2.2 ms worst (first frame).
- N = 48, `u = 1`: .13 ms average, .4 ms worst.

Canvas drawing has not been profiled in a browser. It draws up to 1128 lines in 8 batched paths, or 2256 cells in 2 paths.

## Tests

`model.test.ts`:

- deterministic replay;
- signs are symmetric, ±1 with a zero diagonal, and the unbalanced tally matches a brute-force count;
- a resolve event's triangle was unbalanced;
- `p = .3` gives exactly two camps on three seeds (every sign equals the product of the camp labels, minority ≥ 8);
- the same seeds at `p = .7` give universal friendship;
- raising `p` under unrest dissolves the camps while keeping it low does not;
- lowering `p` under unrest splits a peaceful group;
- one cross-camp reconciliation brings peace at `.7` but heals at `.3`;
- an outcast rejoins a camp but stays alone in peace;
- newcomer, flip and tension bounds hold.

`views.test.ts`:

- every view stays inside desktop and phone frames;
- the circle puts the camps on opposite halves;
- the matrix keeps each camp as one contiguous diagonal block.

## Unresolved

- Whether the initial tangle, at up to 40 flashes per second, is legible or only a burst. Settling takes 13–20 s at the default.
- Peace splits slowly under unrest (minority 4–8 after three minutes). A participant may need to isolate people to see camps re-form.
- Camp labels from the eigenvector can flicker for a person mid-crossing in the circle and matrix views.
