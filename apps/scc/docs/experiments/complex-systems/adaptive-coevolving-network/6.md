# Route 6: bounded confidence with rewiring

Route: `/adaptive-coevolving-network/6`. Date: 2026-10-02.
Code: `apps/scc/components/complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/6/`.

## Model card

- **Object and boundary.** A synthetic population of 240 people (cap 480) with continuous opinions `x ∈ [0, 1]` on an undirected simple graph (random, mean degree 6, everyone tied at least once). It is a mathematical demonstrator of bounded-confidence opinion dynamics on an adaptive network, not an empirical claim about any real public.
- **Rules.** Each tie is activated at rate `activation = 1` per model time unit. An activation picks one end `i` at random; `j` is the other end and `d = x_j − x_i`.
  - `|d| < ε`: compromise (Deffuant et al. 2000): `x_i ← x_i + μd`, `x_j ← x_j − μd`. Stubborn ends do not move.
  - `|d| ≥ ε`: with probability `w`, `i` cuts the tie and ties to a uniformly random person it does not already know (Kozma & Barrat 2008); up to 12 draws, else nothing. Otherwise nothing.
  - Noise: each non-stubborn person resets to a uniformly random opinion at rate `η` (noisy bounded confidence, Pineda, Toral & Hernández-García 2009). Without it the system freezes into absorbing clusters; with it, a jumper appears every ~5 s on screen and has to find a group again, so the network never stops rewiring. `η = .002` was tried first and rejected: its constant flow of jumpers kept re-bridging the groups, and the network never came apart (largest piece ≥ .86 even at `w = 1`).
- **Integration.** Steps of at most `.05` units; tie activations are accumulated as `ties × activation × Δt` and executed one by one, so the activation count is deterministic. The xorshift state lives in the network, so a seed replays exactly.
- **Parameters (defaults).** `ε = .15` (slider `.05–.5`), `w = .3` (slider `0–1`), `μ = .3`, `η = .0005`, 1.5 model units per second on screen (`.45` with reduced motion).
- **Stubborn people.** Participant-made: a held person, a tapped person, or a tapped newcomer. They keep their opinion and are exempt from noise, but still pull others within `ε` and still cut and rewire ties.
- **Macro observables.** The number of opinion groups (sorted opinions split at gaps `> .04`, groups `≥ 4%` of people), the share of ties joining people beyond each other's tolerance, and the connected pieces of the network.

## Screen encoding

- White field. Colour is opinion on one ramp, indigo (0) → magenta → orange (1), with lightness rising monotonically.
- Ties within tolerance are faint ink. Ties beyond tolerance are strong ink, because only they can be cut. A cut leaves a dashed trace to the abandoned person while the new tie draws out toward the stranger (.9 s).
- Node area grows with degree. Stubborn people have an ink ring. While a person is held, a bracket on the opinion axis spans their `±ε`, showing who can still move them.
- Views (옵션 → 보기). A switch eases every point from its snapshot to its live target over .9 s (cubic in-out), then points follow at rate 12/s.
  - *의견 (default):* horizontal position is opinion and vertical position is the force layout's `y`. Switching to and from 네트워크 is purely sideways motion: people slide to what they think. Groups read as vertical strands, and intolerant ties as long diagonals between them.
  - *네트워크:* the force layout of the current ties (springs, O(n²) repulsion, gravity), coloured by opinion. Rewiring shows as lobes of one colour.
  - *궤적:* everyone sits on the top row at their opinion. Ink trails, sampled every .25 s, show each person's opinions over the last 30 s, flowing down: the classic bounded-confidence plot with time running down. Labels read 지금 / 30초 전. Ties and cut marks are hidden here.
- Layout is presentation only and never feeds back into the model.

## Interaction

- **Drag a person sideways:** they become stubborn and hold the opinion `start + Δx / axis width`. The mapping is the same in every view, and in 의견 and 궤적 the person stays under the finger. On release they return to their former stubbornness.
- **Tap a person:** toggles stubbornness.
- **Tap empty space:** adds a stubborn newcomer whose opinion is the horizontal position on the axis, tied to the three nearest people on screen. The cap is 480.
- **Keyboard on the focused canvas:** ←/→ hold the person nearest the centre and move them by .02; Esc or blur releases them; Enter/Space adds a stubborn newcomer, alternating between 0 and 1.
- **Options:** a 옵션/닫기 toggle with a short Korean model description, the views, and two sliders, 좁게–넓게 (`ε`) and 유지–끊기 (`w`).
- **Accessibility:** a hint appears until the first interaction. An sr-only summary every 2 s gives the groups, the largest group's share, the intolerant-tie share and the number of network pieces.

## Measured, pure model only

All runs use 240 people, default parameters (`η = .0005`) and seed `0x5bd1e995`; seeds 1–3 are added where noted.

- **Groups vs tolerance, `w = .3`, t = 60 units** (seeds `0x5bd1e995`, 1, 2, 3):

  | `ε` | Groups |
  | --- | --- |
  | `.05` | 8–10 |
  | `.1` | 4–5 |
  | `.15` | 2–3 |
  | `.2` | 2 |
  | `.3` | 1–2 (1 in three of four) |

  This roughly follows `1/(2ε)`.
- **Rewiring sorts and splits the network, `ε = .15`, t = 90:**

  | `w` | Ties beyond tolerance | Pieces | Largest piece |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `0` | 52% | 1 | 1.00 |
  | `.3` | .4% | 4 | .99 |
  | `.6` | .1% | 5 | .65 |
  | `1` | 0 | 7 | .55 |

  Over 30–90 units with seeds 1–2, the time-averaged largest piece is .72–.93 at `w = .3` and .52–.72 at `w = 1`. Without noise, `w = .3` reaches .37 by t = 120. The pieces each hold one opinion group; the test bounds the opinion spread inside the largest piece below .3.
- **Without rewiring, groups fail to form at low `ε`:** with `w = 0`, 73% (`ε = .1`) and 89% (`ε = .05`) of ties stay intolerable and opinions stay scattered, because each person's few neighbours are mostly out of reach. With `w ≥ .1`, crisp strands form within about 20 units. On a sparse graph, rewiring is what lets people find someone to compromise with.
- **Tempo:** cuts at `w = .3` run at 119 per unit at first, 36 at t = 10, 15 at t = 20, and 1–3 per unit after t = 30. On screen, the first ~10 s are a dense burst while the network sorts itself; after that, individual cuts are visible, mostly when a jumper is cut away.
- **Hysteresis:** once the network has split, widening `ε` rarely reunites it. After a split at `w = .6`, setting `ε = .5` left two groups .47–.54 apart for 120 units (all three seeds); the pieces sometimes rejoined topologically through jumpers, but stayed beyond reach of each other. A stubborn newcomer placed between them and tied into both (the tap gesture) produced one group by t ≈ 80–120 in two of the three seeds. Lowering `w` to 0 freezes the topology entirely.
- **A stubborn person:** the hub of an 86-person group, with `ε = .15` and `w = .3`, held for 50 units.
  - Held .1 away, within tolerance, they drew the group 94% of the way to them, and their degree went from 13 to 14.
  - Held .25 away, beyond tolerance, the group moved only 28% of the way, and every tie was cut (degree 13 to 0).
  - With `η = .002`, dragging a 77-person group's hub gradually (.02–.3 per unit) did not drag the group.

## Tests

`model.test.ts` covers:

- deterministic replay;
- conservation of tie count and simplicity under rewiring;
- compromise only within tolerance, conserving the pair's mean;
- that cuts only remove ties that were beyond tolerance;
- the causal contrast for tolerance: 4 or more groups at `ε = .1`, 2 or fewer at `.3`;
- the causal contrast for rewiring: intolerant ties above .4 at `w = 0`, below .1 at `w = .6`;
- fragmentation: one piece at `w = 0` and a largest piece below .85 at `w = 1` (t = 90), with opinions inside it spanning less than .3;
- a stubborn person within tolerance moves their group, while one beyond tolerance is cut off;
- that noise spares stubborn people;
- bounds and the cap.

`views.test.ts` covers frame bounds for every view at desktop and phone size, that 의견 differs from 네트워크 only horizontally and is monotone in opinion, and that the axis maps both ways.

## Unresolved

- Whether 240 dots in tight strands read as groups on a phone, or whether stacked dots hide group size. The 궤적 view partly answers this.
- Hysteresis: a participant who widens `ε` after the split may expect the groups to merge, and they usually do not. Should the slider reach past .5, or is this the lesson?
- Whether to add an optional opinion-similar rewiring rule (rewire to someone within `ε`), which fragments the network much more clearly.
- Whether the opening burst of cuts should be slowed further.
- The browser frame cost of 궤적 at 480 people.
