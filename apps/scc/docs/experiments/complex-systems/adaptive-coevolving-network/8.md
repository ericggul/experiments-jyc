# Adaptive coevolving network / 8: autocatalytic-set ecosystem

Route: `/adaptive-coevolving-network/8`. Date: 2026-10-02. Component: `components/complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/8/` (`AutocatalyticEcosystemEight`).

## Model card

- **Object and boundary.** A mathematical demonstrator: the Jain–Krishna model of species that catalyse one another's production. It makes no claim about real chemistry or ecology. A fixed number of slots `s = 60` each hold one species; the catalytic graph and populations are the whole system.
- **State.** A directed graph `C` with `c_ij = 1` when species `j` catalyses `i` (link `j → i`), no self-links. Relative populations `x_i ≥ 0`, `Σx = 1`.
- **Fast rule (populations follow links).** `ẋ_i = Σ_j c_ij x_j − x_i Σ_k Σ_j c_kj x_j`. Before every graph update, `x` is set to the attractor of this equation:
  - The solution is `y = e^{Ct} y₀` normalized. It concentrates on species downstream of the strongly connected components with the largest Perron eigenvalue `λ₁`. If several such components are chained, only the most downstream ones count (Jordan growth `t^k e^{λ₁t}`).
  - Implementation: Tarjan components. Each component's `λ` is 0 for a single node and 1 for a simple cycle; otherwise it comes from power iteration on `C + I` with Collatz–Wielandt bounds (tolerance `1e-9`), cached by the component's exact links. The support is the set of nodes whose depth (most dominant components on a path into them) is maximal. On that support, `x` is the Perron vector from power iteration warm-started at the previous `x`; elsewhere `x = 0` exactly.
  - With no cycle (`λ₁ = 0`), only the ends of the longest paths survive, weighted by `C^L 1` (the number of longest paths reaching each one).
- **Slow rule (links follow populations).** The species with the least `x` is removed. Ties, including all zeros, are broken at random within a relative tolerance of `1e-6`. A newcomer with a new identity takes its slot. Each link to and from every other species exists independently with `p = m/(s−1)`.
- **Roles.** *Core*: species in the dominant components (when `λ₁ ≥ 1`). *Periphery*: other species with `x > 0`. *Absent*: `x = 0`. `s₁` is the number of species with `x > 0`. When `λ₁ ≥ 1`, the living species form an autocatalytic set: each has a living catalyst (tested).
- **Loop.** `links → populations → who is least fit → which species is replaced → links`. A cycle that forms by chance captures the population. Every other species then has zero population and is first in line for removal. Newcomers survive only if a living species catalyses them, so the set grows until `s₁ = s`. After that the least-fit species is a living one. If it is a core species that the rest depend on (a keystone), the set crashes.
- **Defaults.** `s = 60`, `m = .5` (range `.15–1.2`), seed `9`, 8 graph updates per second (range 2–16). Reduced motion runs at 0.3× and draws no event marks.

## Screen

- White field. The force layout takes springs along links (direction ignored), O(n²) repulsion and gravity, and is computed in the browser; the model never reads it. Unlinked species drift to the rim, and a growing set pulls its members together.
- Dot area grows with `x_i` (an average share `1/s` draws at about 2.5× the base radius). Core species are green (`rgb(18,122,82)`), periphery ink, and absent species hollow ink rings. Drawn sizes ease toward the new attractor at rate 6/s, so a crash reads as many dots deflating together.
- Each link `j → i` is a wedge, wide at the catalyst and pointed at the species it feeds, with width set by the catalyst's population (`c_ij x_j` is the flow). Links out of absent species are faint, and links inside the core are green.
- Event marks, .9 s each:
  - a replacement leaves the departed species's links dashed while the newcomer's links draw out, with a ring at the slot (larger for a participant's extinction);
  - every species whose population drops to zero gets a ring closing in on it.
- No title, counters or legend. The sr-only summary updates every 2 s with `s₁`, core size and `λ₁`, or says that no autocatalytic set exists yet.

## Measured, pure model (Node, 2026-10-02)

- **Regimes** (`s = 60`, 3000 updates, seeds `7919·k`, k = 1…6):

  | `m` | first ACS (updates, mean) | `s₁ ≥ s−1` first reached | share of time full | crashes (drop ≥ .3 s) per 1000 updates |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | .25 | 622 | 1406 (5/6 runs) | .17 | 1.06 |
  | .4 | 119 | 621 | .60 | 1.28 |
  | .6 | 76 | 349 | .87 | .28 |
  | .8 | 44 | 168 | .94 | .11 |
  | 1.2 | 3 | 40 | .99 | 0 |

  Sparse links mean a long random phase and a fragile set. Dense links mean an almost immediate, robust set. `m ≈ .4–.6` shows the full cycle of random phase, growth, crash and recovery.
- **Mean `s₁` over 600 updates** (seeds 1, 2, 3, 9, 10):
  - `m = .15`: 2–3, with no ACS in four of the five runs;
  - `m = .5`: 22–48;
  - `m = .9`: 49–58.
- **Default story** (seed 9, `m = .5`, 8/s): `s₁` was 3 at 0 s, 21 at 20 s, 51 at 30 s and 60 at 40 s. It crashed to 42 at 60 s and 14 at 70 s, then recovered to 60 by 90 s.
- **Keystones** (state after 300 updates; every living species removed in turn on a clone):

  | seed | `s₁` | `λ₁` | core species | mean core loss | worst core loss | keystones (loss ≥ .3 `s₁`) | mean periphery loss |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 9 | 59 | 1.43 | 8 | 11.4 | 38 | 2 | .8 |
  | 1 | 59 | 1.19 | 5 | 54 | 54 | 5 | 1.1 |
  | 3 | 59 | 1.58 | 16 | 10.8 | 32 | 4 | .7 |

  A larger, more interconnected core (higher `λ₁`) has fewer keystones relative to its size.
- **Cost:**
  - Graph update: mean .1–.15 ms at `s = 60–80`, p99 under 1 ms. Rare spikes of 5–25 ms (p999 at most 4 ms) come from slowly converging power iterations on large supports, up to about 5000 iterations.
  - Model plus layout per 60 fps frame at 16 updates/s: mean .02 ms, p99 .13 ms, worst .6 ms (seed 9, 120 s).
  - Not profiled in a browser.

## Tests

`model.test.ts`:

- deterministic replay;
- `Σx = 1`, `x ≥ 0`, `x > 0` exactly when not absent, no self-links, and consistent adjacency lists;
- an autocatalytic property when `λ₁ ≥ 1`;
- `Cx = λ₁x` on the living species, with `λ₁` matching an independent whole-graph power iteration;
- the acyclic case keeps only path ends;
- each update replaces a least-populated species with one new identity;
- linkage contrast: mean `s₁` is below 10 at `m = .15`, above 30 at `.5`, and above 45 at `.9`;
- keystone contrast: on a clone, removing the worst core species costs at least 30% of `s₁`, and the mean core loss exceeds 3× the mean periphery loss;
- removal bounds.

`views.test.ts`: every view keeps species and labels inside the frame at 1440×760 and 390×700, and the 역할 view orders core, then periphery, then absent outward.

## Unresolved

- Whether a participant can find keystones by eye. Core species are green, but which of them is a keystone is not encoded; it is meant to be discovered by trial.
- Rare 5–25 ms update spikes. They could be removed with a better eigen-solver or by spreading the work across frames.
- Ties among equal positive populations use a `1e-6` relative tolerance on a numerically converged vector. Exact symmetries could in principle be broken by numerical noise.
- The papers use `s = 100` and `m ≈ .25`, with thousands of updates per epoch. This route compresses that to `s = 60`, `m = .5`, so the random phase is shorter than in the source figures.
