# Amoeba / 2

Route: `/amoeba/2`. A standalone clone of [amoeba/1](README.md), added
2026-10-06. Code: `components/complex-systems/amoeba/2`; it shares no imports
with `/1`.

## Changed variables

1. **Zone instead of dish.** The lawn fills the screen. When the colony is
   created, the zone takes the viewport's aspect (clamped to 0.5–3) as
   [-halfWidth, halfWidth] × [-1, 1]. Its walls are the screen edges, and
   bodies bounce off them. A later resize cover-fits the same zone, so the
   frame stays full-bleed and crops instead of reshaping the model.
2. **Inherited, widely varying body size k.**
   - Founders draw k from a Pareto tail, P(k > x) = (0.5 / x)^1.3, clamped to
     [0.5, 6]: most are small and a few are huge.
   - At each division each daughter's k drifts multiplicatively, log k plus a
     normal(0, 0.25) step, so one lineage spreads over sizes within a few
     generations.
   - Sizes are heterogeneous in the way scale-free degree sequences are, but
     no preferential-attachment rule is modelled. Compare
     [Barabási–Albert / 1](../barabasi-albert/README.md).

## Size scaling

A newborn has mass k². Intake, cost, and the division and cyst thresholds all
scale with k², so size changes how large and how fast a body is, not whether
it can live. Larger bodies are slower, with speed proportional to k^-0.35.

- **Grazing:** a body grazes every food square under its footprint, and the
  mass-proportional intake is drawn evenly from them.
- **Division (2026-10-06 rewrite).** Division conserves mass and may be
  unequal.
  - When division begins, the cell picks a split fraction: 0.5 ± 0.18,
    bounded to [0.25, 0.75].
  - The halves keep split · M and (1 − split) · M. Their radii are √split and
    √(1 − split) of the parent's, at centres 0.9 · (rA + rB) apart, placed so
    the centre of mass stays (`splitGeometry`).
  - The dividing shape in the shader uses the same split and geometry, so the
    end of division matches the two new bodies exactly.
  - Size variety now comes from unequal divisions accumulating over
    generations. Each half's inherited target size k only sets its next
    division threshold. It drifts by log-normal(0.15) and is bounded so a half
    is neither already due to divide nor already starving.
  - A cell cycle is at least 15 ticks.
  - **Why:** the earlier version reset each half's mass to a re-drawn k², so
    sizes jumped at every division, and it always drew equal halves, so the
    shape snapped when division ended.
  - Tests: mass conservation; drawn half radii equal to the model's; centre of
    mass kept; no body's radius changes more than 5 % in one tick.
- **Recovery:** `wakeChance` is 2·10⁻⁵ and cyst lifetime 6,000 ticks.
  - With a lifetime of 4,000 ticks, cysts dissolved before their eaten squares
    regrew to the wake level (about 4,600 ticks), and a single founder went
    extinct.
  - With `wakeChance` 2·10⁻⁶, 900 cysts expected only about 2.5 spontaneous
    wakes per window.
- **Contacts:**
  - Each touching pair is resolved once, from the larger body's side, so a
    search spans only that body's diameter.
  - The lighter body gives way more, but no body is shoved more than 12 % of
    its own radius per tick.
  - Without that cap a giant splitting next to a tiny body flung it by up to
    1.25 of its own radii in a frame, a velocity change of 0.70 radii per
    frame². With the cap these are 0.32 and 0.096.
- **Cyst wake-up:** the contact pass also marks cysts that an active body
  touches, which replaces a per-cyst neighbour search. That search had to
  reach the largest body's radius.
- **Cyst removal:** compaction moves surviving runs with `copyWithin`.
  Before, mass dissolutions caused steps of 4–7 ms.

Base radius 0.022 (at 0.03 the boom and bust went extinct), cyst lifetime
6,000 ticks, capacity 4,096.

## Evidence (headless, seed 7, 16:9, no browser)

`model/index.test.ts` asserts:
- determinism;
- division keeping the lineage and an equal fill;
- free-medium placement inside the zone;
- a 95th / 5th percentile radius ratio above 4 within 900 ticks (observed
  7.6–9.9× in sheets);
- bodies staying in the zone;
- at least two blooms in 12,000 ticks without extinction or capacity hits.

`rendering/bodies.test.ts` bounds drawn motion in each body's own radii.

The mean model step is about 1–1.4 ms. Occasional 4–11 ms steps land at
different ticks on repeated runs of the same seed, so they are runtime noise,
not a model hotspot.

## Rendering

Same three layers as `/1` (spring bodies, summed field, owner pass, gel
composite), with these changes:

- The dish is removed.
- The field pass also accumulates a field-weighted radius, so normals,
  refraction depth and shadows scale smoothly where a giant meets a tiny body.
- Granules keep one absolute size, so big bodies hold many more.
- Nuclei shrink relative to large bodies.
- Food and grain textures span the zone.

The user reported that the route would not load. The investigation found:

- **The 500 response.** While the report was investigated, every route returned
  HTTP 500. The cause was another task's in-progress `traffic-light/1` compile
  error: an import of a missing `ARM_LAYOUTS` export. Turbopack reports
  compile errors for all routes. It cleared when that file was fixed.
- **Loading.** With a working server, DOMContentLoaded took 64 ms. Colony
  creation took 1–3 ms, renderer creation and the grain bake 8–16 ms, and
  shader compile plus the first frame 15–30 ms. No console errors.
- **GPU time per frame.** These were measured with
  `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`. The tab was occluded because the display
  was asleep, so CPU timings were distorted by background throttling and are
  not reported.

  | State | GPU per frame |
  | --- | --- |
  | First version, about 1,470 cells | 4.5–25 ms |
  | After the changes below, about 680 cells at the first bloom | about 5 ms (field 2.2, owner 2.4, composite 1.5) |
  | After the changes below, about 2,050 cells | about 3 ms typical, 12 ms worst |

- **The changes.**
  - The composite normal comes from `dFdx`/`dFdy` instead of 4 field taps.
  - Refraction uses one lawn sample instead of three for dispersion.
  - The shadow uses one tap.
  - The cyst lifetime is 4,000 ticks. Steady-state cysts fell from about
    3,000 to about 600, and the mean model step fell from 1.4 to 0.44 ms.

## Frame pacing (2026-10-06)

The user reported visible stepping as the colony grew. The 24 fps cap does not
divide a 60 Hz display, so frames were held for 2, 3, 2… vsyncs, which reads as
judder even when every frame is fast.

- **Rendering:** `/2` now renders on every vsync. The model keeps 30 ticks/s,
  and the spring bodies interpolate between ticks.
- **Catch-up:** a late frame catches up at most 2 ticks, so one slow frame
  never becomes a burst of simulation work.
- **Food texture:** it is re-blurred and uploaded only on frames where a tick
  happened.
- **Pacing governor** (`rendering/pacing.ts`, tested):
  - If 18 rendered frames arrive more than 1.45× late, the internal
    resolution steps from 1.0 to 0.8 and then 0.65.
  - The last level renders every second vsync, which keeps pacing even.
  - Quality returns after 240 on-time frames.
- **Main-thread CPU** for model plus bodies at 60 fps over 4 simulated
  minutes: median 0.05 ms, p99 1.36 ms, 2 of 14,280 frames over 8 ms.
- **GPU** on the user's MacBook Air (above): 3–12 ms per frame at about 2,000
  cells.
- **GPU-safety deviation:** rendering above 24 Hz is recorded here, with the
  user's authorisation for browser checks. The required 30 s
  foreground record is still missing because the device's display was asleep.
  Until then the governor is the safeguard.

## Resolution (2026-10-06)

Three things the renderer had added kept the image soft or degrading:

1. **A DPR ≤ 1 cap.** On Retina, the frame was rendered at half resolution and
   upscaled.
2. **A pacing governor that lowered internal resolution** (0.8, then 0.65)
   whenever frames were late.
3. **Silhouettes and normals taken bilinearly** from a field at a quarter of
   the output resolution. The threshold contour became a polygon (stair-steps
   when magnified) and the highlights faceted.

The fixes:

- **Output resolution:** the composite now renders at device resolution (DPR
  up to 2, within a 4.5 MP cap).
- **Field and owner passes:** they stay at half the CSS resolution, so their
  cost does not grow with the display.
- **Lawn grain:** baked at the zone's on-screen pixel size.
- **Pacing:** the governor never reduces resolution; when late, it renders
  every second vsync.
- **Reconstruction:** near bodies the composite reconstructs the field with a
  cubic B-spline (4 bilinear fetches per term for the value, d/dx and d/dy).
  The formulas match a direct 4×4 sum to 10⁻¹⁵. Silhouettes are smooth at any
  magnification and normals come from analytic derivatives.
- **Follow-up, on the user's screen (Brave, 1470×956 points, DPR 2):**
  - **The cap was too low.** At full screen the 4.5 MP cap forced a
    fractional ratio of about 1.79, which resamples and softens the frame.
    The cap is now 8.3 MP, which covers 2940×1912 and 3456×2234 Retina
    panels, and only whole-number ratios are used (above the cap, 1).
  - **The owner pass was nearest-sampled at a quarter of output resolution.**
    Creases and interior fades showed in 4×4-pixel blocks, more of them as
    the colony grew. It is now linearly filtered for the distance channel,
    with body ids read exactly by `texelFetch`.
- **Measured GPU** at 2400×1424 (DPR 2, about 2,000 cells), by timer query:
  median field 2.1, owner 2.2, composite 4.0 ms, about 8.3 ms per frame in
  total.
