## Tested relation

Two dozen windows the size of a person's browser windows pour in over a few
seconds and stay, covering the desktop. As soon as their pages have loaded,
one attention flicks among them for twenty seconds: a window stays in front
for a fifth of a second or so, long enough to catch a headline or a face,
then another; it goes back to the one it just left a quarter of the time,
rushes through three to six in a flurry now and then, snags on one for most
of a second while scrolling it, and once in a while drags a window a little
or rearranges a few. It never pauses and nobody reads. The material is the
fear of missing out around AI: the jobs it will take, the stocks that will
moon, the graduates nobody hires, the courses everyone else has finished,
the forecast that became a deadline. Some windows are real pages, some are
cloned services, and some are just a picture or a video opened on its own.

Question: does a desktop of ordinary-sized windows, flicked through at the
pace of nervous checking, read as one anxious attention rather than as many
pages or as a strobe? Baseline: hype/1, whose windows arrive over minutes
and are read. Earlier versions of this experiment (2026-10-10 to -11) had
tiered sizes from a fifth of the desktop to the whole of it and 15–120 ms
flicks with windows teleporting on every third flick, and were judged the
worst state so far: too many windows covering the screen, too many tiny
ones, sizes nobody's browser has, and nothing to feel.

## How it works

- **Burst** (`model/rhythm.ts`): `gap` (0–1500 ms, default 250, varied
  0.4–1.8×) opens the windows one after another, 24 in six to seven
  seconds; 0 launches them all at the same instant, which places them
  within a tenth of a second but stalls the machine (measured below:
  at 120 ms the Mac was unresponsive for about six seconds and the pages
  finished loading between 7 and 14 s; at 300 ms the stall was mild and
  the pages finished between 1 and 12 s). The surface opens the first,
  waits until the instance accepts hand-offs (about a second cold), then
  the rest. No limit: all stay; 6–40 of them, default 24.
- **Flicking** (`plan.ts`): a 10 ms loop raises a random window among
  those whose page has loaded (`document.readyState` asked through the
  page's DevTools session every 400 ms, or 8 s after placement at the
  latest), with `Target.activateTarget`, the browser-level command that
  reorders windows (`Page.bringToFront` only activates a tab and was the
  reason the first version did not visibly flick). It starts as soon as
  two pages have loaded, so there is no dead time, and the `duration`
  seconds (5–300, default 20) start counting once three quarters of the
  windows have loaded (25 s after the start at the latest). A window
  stays in front for a dwell log-uniform from `pace`/3 to 2.2 × `pace` ms
  (pace 60–1000, default 220: 73–484 ms, median about 190); a quarter of
  the flicks go back to the window that was just in front; 15 % start a
  flurry of three to six flicks 45–75 ms apart; 12 % snag, staying
  500–900 ms while the page scrolls a few hundred pixels; 30 % of the
  others scroll it a little. `moves` % of flicks (0–60, default 8) drag
  the window a little (up to 12 % of the desktop) and perhaps resize it
  (up to 8 %), and every 5–9 s two to four windows are rearranged to
  fresh places and sizes. There are no pauses. The loop ends by itself
  and tells the control (`done`); the windows remain. Cost per flick is
  one DevTools command, sometimes three.
- **Windows** (`model/layout.ts`): the windows look like a person's
  browser windows on a laptop. Widths spread in a bell around `size`
  (50–85 % of the visible desktop's width, default 70; the bell reaches
  ±16 points, never below 45 % or above 85 %); heights are a bell around
  80 % of the desktop's height (±14 points, following the width's
  deviation, never below 58 % or above 93 %), so no ordinary window covers
  the desktop and none is a strip or a thumbnail. One window in
  twenty-five is the whole desktop, as a zoomed window is, and a run has
  at most one of those. A window opens flush with the top 35 % of the
  time and with the left or right edge 20 % each, otherwise anywhere;
  two dozen of them cover the desktop (checked on a coarse grid in the
  tests). Pages are shown at 100 % by default; the option `scaled` zooms
  pages out in narrow windows (1024 pt reference, never below 0.75). A
  share is forced dark (default 30 %).
- **Deck** (`model/catalogue.ts`): 63 real pages and 94 clones over the
  topics AI jobs, AI stocks, AI layoffs, learn AI, CS graduates, AGI 2027,
  AI bubble, NVIDIA, AI tools, prompt engineering, AI 자격증, AI 수혜주, AI 대체
  직업. Real: Google News and Naver news searches for each fear, YouTube
  searches, 18 YouTube videos found through YouTube's own search for the
  FOMO queries (titles and channels confirmed through oEmbed), Layoffs.fyi,
  Will Robots Take My Job, AI 2027, Situational Awareness, Metaculus, Yahoo
  Finance and TradingView NVDA, Naver's mobile quote page, 원티드/사람인/잡코리아
  job searches, Hacker News jobs, Coursera, YC's AI companies, the WEF jobs
  report, Epoch, Our World in Data, Google Trends and two Wikipedia
  articles (LinkedIn, Indeed, Udemy, Product Hunt, There's An AI For That
  and Reddit were removed after their walls and challenges, see below). Clones: Slack, Signal article, Medium listicle ("10 jobs that
  won't exist by 2027"), LinkedIn Jobs ("over 100 applicants", entry level
  0 results), a trading-app quote page (NVDA or SK하이닉스, green chart, AI
  watchlist), a Blind post (팀 절반 정리, 컴공 백수), Google results, YouTube
  watch pages around the real videos, landing pages (AI 자격증 2주 완성,
  prompt engineering bootcamp), 17 image files (Wikipedia lead images of
  Huang, Altman, Musk, Amodei, Zuckerberg, a data centre, Nvidia's campus, a
  pharmacy robot, Optimus, an unemployment chart, a crash chart, and stills
  of the videos named like screenshots) shown in Chrome's own image viewer,
  and the videos opened on their own, full-window.
- **Reader**: none. The cloned pages keep their composers but nobody writes.

Parameters: windows 6–40 (24), gap 0–1500 ms (250), for (duration, 5–300,
default 20), dwell (pace, 60–1000 ms, 220), moves 0–60 % (8), typical
width 50–85 % (70), pages (at 100 % or zoomed out when narrow), cloned
pages 0–100 % (60), dark pages 0–100 % (30), sound, pattern, clear before
a run.

## Evidence

Pure tests (`model/model.test.ts`, 6): burst order and gap bounds, the
dwell distribution, the chain's topics, the window sizes (containment, the
width and height bounds, most widths within eight points of the typical
width, variety, the share of whole-desktop windows, edges touched, two
dozen covering a coarse grid, a nudge staying on the desktop and small),
deck well-formedness (unique IDs, https for real pages, pages, images and
videos that exist, no repeats at 100 % clones, only real at 0 %), composed
runs on three displays with no zoom by default and 0.75–1 when scaled, at
most one whole-desktop window in twenty seeds, and the quote chart's
reproducibility. They pass with hype/1's and the helper's;
`pnpm --filter @scc/archive typecheck` passes; ESLint reports no errors
(warnings: `<img>` and the Google Fonts link).

## Open questions

- Whether the dwell of about a fifth of a second with returns, flurries
  and snags reads as nervous checking; the dwell and the snag share are
  the first things to tune.
- Whether moved windows should animate (DevTools moves jump) and whether a
  move should ever send a window partly off-screen.
- Whether the cookie bars and permission prompts should be accepted in
  the profile beforehand or kept as the real desktop's noise.
- Which fears are missing: housing, dating, the next keyword after AI.
