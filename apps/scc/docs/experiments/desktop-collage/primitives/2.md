# primitives/2 — entangled windows

Route: `/desktop-collage/primitives/2`, created 2026-10-05. Window page:
`/desktop-collage/primitives/2/window`. Implementation:
`components/desktop-collage/primitives/2/`.

## Tested relation

Each Chrome app window holds one part of a single desktop-wide scene, framed
by the window's own position on the screen. Bring windows close, by hand or by
letting them drift, and their clouds connect. The window is used as a piece of
cut paper over one continuous image: papier collé made from browser windows.

## Sources

  by [Bjørn Staal](https://www.nonfigurativ.com/projects/entangled), 2023, MIT.
  Read from `main.js` and `WindowManager.js`, and kept:
  - an orthographic camera in screen coordinates whose offset follows the

    per frame (the same decay applied per 24 Hz tick);
  - one clock for every window: seconds since local midnight;

    `HSL(i × 0.1, 1, 0.5)`, rotating x 0.5t, y 0.3t, on black, with the
    camera's depth range `±10000`.
- **Look: [*Entangled*](https://www.fxhash.xyz/vertex/entangled)**, the
  particle work. It was a
  [2025 Lumen Prize Hybrid Award finalist](https://lumenprize.org/2025-hybrid-award-finalists/bjorn-staal),
  and its first two-window clip went viral on 2023-11-22. It is closed source.
  The **clouds** form follows frames of that clip, shown as
  [reported coverage](https://shazoo.ru/2023/11/30/150641/programmist-predstavil-vpecatliaiushhii-mnogookonnyi-adaptivnyi-interfeis):
  - a veined particle shell in the window's colour;
  - a core in the partner window's colour (green shell / red core, and the
    mirror);
  - an hourglass bridge of two intertwined streams;
  - dust, additive blending, near-black navy background.
  The open clone [ore-codes/entangled](https://github.com/ore-codes/entangled)

What this app adds: it opens and places the real windows itself
(`foundations/surfaces/chrome-app`), and can drift them (`foundations/control`
`animate`), so the machine performs the connections.

## Fidelity limit

The original clip used about 3 million particles. Without observation on this
device, the repository's [GPU safety](../../../../../../docs/harness/webgpu-tsl-particles.md)
budget allows 8,192 visible particles at DPR 1 and 24 Hz. Within it, particles
are concentrated on the veins and the bridge takes 20%; the clouds still read
sparser than the original. A higher budget needs the documented 30-second
observation on this Mac.

## How it works

- **Sync:** windows announce their page and frame rectangles over a same-origin
  `BroadcastChannel` at most 24 times a second (plus a 400 ms heartbeat). A
  window silent for 1.5 s is forgotten. The channel opens per mount, so
  development StrictMode remounts are safe.
- **Rendering:** React Three Fiber with an orthographic camera framed on the
  window's page area in desktop points.
  - Cloud layers are static point sets on vein ridges, rotated and breathing
    in the vertex shader.
  - The bridge profile and flow are computed in the shader from uniforms.
  - No buffer is rewritten per frame. React re-renders only when a window joins
    or leaves.
- **Budget** ([GPU safety](../../../../../../docs/harness/webgpu-tsl-particles.md)):
  DPR 1; a demand frame loop capped at 24 Hz; paused while the page is hidden.
  Points per window ≤ 4,096 for spheres plus ≤ 2,048 for links, so ≤ 6,144.
  Reduced motion freezes the field's own motion and redraws only when a window
  moves.
- **Pages:** served by this app. The throwaway Chrome profile therefore runs
  with `--ignore-certificate-errors` (its warning bar is hidden with
  `--test-type`).

Parameters: form (entangled clouds or source cubes), windows 2–8, opening
(row, ring, scatter), window size, reach (% of desktop width), colours
(green + red as in the reference, or pink + red), and motion (by hand, or
drifting by itself with amplitude and cycle).

## Form: evolving network (2026-10-06)

The first form tailored beyond the reference. Each window runs its own
evolving acquaintance network (`model/acquaintance.ts`): the
Davidsen–Ebel–Bornholdt rules of
[self-evolving-network/1](../../complex-systems/self-evolving-network/README.md).
A person with two or more acquaintances introduces two of them, closing a
triangle; one with fewer meets a stranger; with probability *turnover*
(2–30%, default 6%) someone leaves, their ties vanish, and a newcomer arrives.
The rate is one update per person per second; populations are 24–72,
scaled by window area.

Across windows (the change being tested):
- acquaintances can live in another window;
- a person who knows someone elsewhere introduces a local acquaintance to
  them, as often as the windows are near, so triangles close across windows;
- windows that touch also meet directly (6% per update at full strength);
- ties across windows dissolve as windows part (1.2% per update when near,
  up to 9.2% when far) and at once when the other person leaves.

Each window simulates only its own people and shares a snapshot of its graph
12 times a second. Every window draws every window's network where that
window is, plus the ties across windows. Layout (`model/layout.ts`,
Fruchterman–Reingold with a bounded step) pulls people with ties elsewhere to
the edge facing that window.

Encoding:
- people grow with acquaintances (both kinds) and turn from white to their
  window's colour with tenure;
- ties inside a window are faint;
- ties across windows are bright, coloured from one window's colour to the
  other's, with pulses travelling along them, a flash when new, and beads
  passing both ways.

Tests: deterministic replay; triangles and turnover inside a window; ties
across windows form when near, dissolve when far, and never outlive the other
person; they stay a minority even when windows touch (30 people: 6 reaching
across); people reaching across gather at the facing edge.

Capture through DevTools after 12 s, 2 windows: green and red networks, ties
across with beads, people gathered at the facing edges. Isolated people drift
to the page margins, a layout question still open.

### Network as gel, and performance (2026-10-06, later)

**Rendering.** The network is now drawn as one flat 2D gel, ported from
[adaptive-coevolving-network/1-glsl](../../complex-systems/adaptive-coevolving-network/1-glsl.md)
(`window/gel.ts`):
- people are discs and ties are curved, tapering ribbons; each adds `exp(−d/k)`
  of its signed distance into one float field;
- a composite takes `−k · ln Σ`, so ties fillet into people and the network
  reads as one body;
- shaders, `thin` taper and constants are the source's;
- positions are desktop points, and each window draws from its own place, so
  the gel continues across windows;
- ties across windows take the source's travelling swell and brightening
  (there: ties across a disagreement), two-coloured, about 3× thicker in the
  middle, and thicker still while new;
- the earlier lines, points and beads are gone.

**Performance.**
- Three draw calls per frame (instanced ribbons, instanced discs, composite)
  at 24 Hz, DPR 1.
- Instance data is written in place into preallocated arrays by module-level
  writers: no per-frame objects, string keys or colour objects. Node lookups
  use numeric keys in a reused map.
- The field is RGBA32F where `EXT_float_blend` exists (present on this Mac's
  Chrome), RGBA16F otherwise; the composite clamps the field so a half-float
  overflow cannot become a NaN.
- The model keeps adjacency sets, so neighbour and cross-tie lookups cost a
  person's degree. The layout accumulates forces in typed arrays (its test
  went from 15.9 to 7.5 ms with identical positions).
- Measured in Node on this Mac: 0.011 / 0.026 / 0.077 ms per 24 Hz tick
  (updates plus a layout step) at 24 / 48 / 72 people.
- Snapshots are shared every tick (24 Hz), so other windows' people move as
  smoothly as one's own.

**Observed** through DevTools captures: star-like bodies, tapering processes,
and beaded two-coloured ties entering from the partner window. Tiny dark
notches remain on the far side of some people: each ribbon ends flat at its
person's centre, so the haze behind it breaks. Still open: frame times in a
visible window.

### Continuous society (2026-10-06, later)

Feedback: the network's evolution still looked discrete, ticking and
unstable. Fades layered over the discrete model did not fix it, so the model
was replaced (`model/society.ts`; `model/acquaintance.ts` was removed).

**Diagnosis, measured in Node at 40 people.** The old model ran DEB updates
at 0.2 per person per second with turnover per update. That meant 44
departures and 281 new ties per minute, and every tie a binary spring that
switched on or off in one step. Peak acceleration was 913 px/s², and the
layout never settled. The renderer also seeded each tie's thickness, bend and
sway from its position in the draw list, so any event reshaped every later
tie at once.

**Model: continuous time, continuous strengths.**
- Each tie has a strength w, integrated every frame:
  `dw/dt = ρ·(spark + support)·(1 − w) − δ·(1 + load²)·w`. *spark* is a newly
  met tie's impulse, decaying over 5 s. *support* is continuous triadic
  closure, Σ over shared acquaintances of w_ik·w_kj, after weighted
  social-network models
  ([Kumpula et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.228701)).
  *load* is the two people's mean summed strength over a capacity of 3, a
  limited attention that keeps the network sparse. A tie no triangle holds
  fades by itself; a clustered tie holds. ρ 0.5, δ 0.25.
- Each person has a presence p, rising over 1.6 s on arrival and falling over
  2.4 s on departure. A tie's effective strength is w·p_a·p_b.
- Events only start things at strength zero: introductions (a Poisson process,
  0.06 per person per second, after DEB, with acquaintances chosen in
  proportion to strength) and the start of a departure (turnover × 0.0003 per
  person per second: about nine minutes' stay at the default 6).
- Across windows the same law applies. Support is the windows' nearness;
  extra decay grows as the windows part; the other person's shared presence
  carries the strength.

**Layout and drawing read the same quantities.** Springs are weighted by
effective strength, repulsion by both people's presence, and the pull toward
another window by the strength of the ties into it. The speed cap is 1.5
ideal lengths per second, and the walls are soft. A tie's body is √w, and it
reaches out from one end as w rises through 0.35 and draws back as it falls.
A person's size is their presence and summed strength. Tie seeds come from the
pair's keys. This window is drawn from its live model; other windows'
positions, presence and strengths are eased every frame toward their 24 Hz
snapshots. The earlier grow, withdraw and arrival fades are gone.

**Measured** in Node, 40 people, 2 minutes after a 90 s warm start:
- mean degree about 4 (ties with strength above 0.2);
- about 50 ties begin per minute and none appears or vanishes in one step;
  the largest change in any tie's effective strength is 0.017 per 1/60 s;
- acceleration p99 27 px/s² and peak about 130 px/s²;
- model and layout cost 0.03–0.04 ms per frame at 24–72 people; warm start
  takes under 80 ms.

The test `nothing visible changes in one step` holds that bound. Frames run at
up to 60 Hz and DPR ≤ 2, above the particle budget's 24 Hz / DPR 1 default.
The form draws three instanced passes, not particles, but the 30-second
frame-time observation in a visible window is still owed.
