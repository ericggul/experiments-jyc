# Face trace — /mobile/face-trace/1

2026-09-28. Experimental, device viewing unverified. This forks the local front-camera and MediaPipe Face Landmarker setup from gaze tracking, but changes the visible relation: three moving masks retain the camera's actual pixels inside the outer contours of both eyes and the mouth. Everything else remains black. The whole output is mirrored like a front-camera preview; detection and rendering stay local to the browser. No images are saved or sent.

Camera access starts on a tap. When the face disappears, the image clears. Hiding or leaving the page stops the camera tracks; returning to a visible tab restarts after access was granted. Inference uses one face and reuses the existing local model and Wasm assets. The mask is a landmark estimate, so fast movement, occlusion, glasses, low light, and open or closed mouths may expose imperfect edges. Live mobile camera alignment and performance still need device observation.

2026-09-28 refinement. Each detected camera frame is processed once into a transparent eye-and-mouth image. The raw video is never drawn directly to the visible canvas. The processed frames are then aligned by a smoothed face position, eye span, and tilt, and adjacent frames are briefly blended. The three cutouts and their pixels scale together around the face center: 2× near the viewport center, easing toward 1× as the face moves away. Scale has its own slower smoothing to avoid size jumps. Detection is capped near 15 FPS, canvas drawing near 30 FPS, and canvas area at 1.8 million pixels. These changes aim to reduce jitter and redundant drawing; the absence of unnatural motion is not yet device verified.

## /2 — liquid field

2026-09-28. `/2` is an independent fork of `/1`. Its stabilized camera eyes and mouth stay clear in a transparent foreground canvas. The same three contour-masked regions supply the only image pixels in the full-viewport WebGL2 layer. Each region becomes one texture tile; empty pixels in that tile are extended from the nearest visible eye or mouth pixel, so the field can sample it everywhere without introducing other face areas. The shader continuously warps a shared texture coordinate and morphs between the three tiles according to their tracked positions. `face-voronoi/3` informed the use of a shared coordinate and a WebGL material field; its portrait images, atlas, and visual output are not used here.

The WebGL layer is one context, capped at 600,000 pixels and about 24 draws per second. Reduced-motion preference holds shader time still, while live face position may still move the field. The foreground retains `/1`'s roughly 15 detections and 30 draws per second. WebGL2 availability, shader compilation, frame rate, visual balance, and camera alignment have not been observed on a device for this fork.

## /3 — nested feature scales

2026-09-28. `/3` retains the processed local camera frame and stabilized face pose from `/1`, then draws each eye and the mouth at ten sizes: 1×, 1.5×, 2.1×, 2.9×, 3.9×, 5.1×, 6.35×, 7.6×, 8.8×, and 10×. Every size shares its own feature's center; larger impressions are more transparent and the original is drawn last. There is no WebGL layer. The source frame contains only the three contour-masked regions. Rendering is capped near 24 FPS and 900,000 canvas pixels; camera inference stays near 15 FPS. Device performance and visual alignment remain to be checked on a phone.

## /4 — three-feature gradient

2026-09-28. Independent fork of `/2`. The clear, stabilized camera cutouts of both eyes and the mouth remain. `/face-voronoi/3`'s `face-gradient 3` supplied the operative rule: align image coordinates before blending actual image samples in linear light. `/4` adapts that rule to three live camera patches centered on the eyes and mouth. Each patch retains its local coordinate at its feature center; coordinates gradually align only where the patches overlap. Their combined presence fades smoothly into black outside the feature surroundings. No average RGB color field, contour bands, or all-screen sine distortion remain.

The gradient uses one WebGL2 context capped at 600,000 pixels and about 24 draws per second. The foreground and inference retain the fork's existing limits. On 2026-09-28, the reference `face-gradient 3` and `/mobile/face-trace/4` were viewed in Chrome at the running HTTPS origin; the first all-screen color field was visibly muddy and the first shared-coordinate version produced a V-shaped distortion. A bounded shared-coordinate revision removed that distortion in a 390×844 camera view. Physical phone alignment and frame rate remain unverified.

## /5 — three-source flux

2026-09-28. Independent copy of `/2`. The foreground contour cutouts and camera tracking remain intact. The shader draws pixels from the actual camera image at each eye or mouth edge outward across the viewport. For each background pixel it traces backward through the sum of three outward source fields; where fields meet, their shared direction curves and the elongated image bends with it. The earlier all-screen sine drift and perimeter folding are removed.

The first revision made the result muddy: it erased the source features from the texture, sampled mostly surrounding skin, and averaged the three patches at their meeting regions. It also cleared the foreground on one missed landmark frame. The correction keeps the original camera patches, selects one source per traced field line without color averaging, and holds the last clear foreground frame through brief tracking gaps (up to 1.2 seconds). It introduces no generated colors or tint.

After reviewing the actual `/face-voronoi/3` renderer, `/5` uses its coordinate-first principle, nearest/two-nearest site contact geometry, and smooth union of material bodies: the three-source flux warps the coordinate before site evaluation, the closest two tracked features define a bisector distance, and proximity to that bisector compresses and bends the selected feature's material coordinates. The three extended forms join over black negative space. The selected camera patch remains the sole color source at each pixel. A compressed two-dimensional coordinate moves from the feature edge toward its original interior as the extension travels outward, retaining eye and lip detail instead of spreading surrounding skin or repeating one perimeter pixel. The local atlas tiles are 256 pixels square. The reference's generated hue field and portrait images are not used.

The field is capped at 320,000 pixels and about 20 draws per second with four bounded flux steps. Tracking changes the field; there is no autonomous color or sampling animation. This is a visual approximation of magnetic flux, not a physical magnetic simulation. Camera alignment, shader compilation, motion, and mobile performance have not been checked in a browser or on a device.

## /6 — twelve live feature sets

2026-09-28. Independent copy of `/2`, retaining its liquid WebGL background and `/1`'s live contour-masked camera pixels. The two eyes and mouth are first composed once in a small transparent canvas, centered in the lower half of the portrait viewport, then drawn twelve times at 30° intervals around the viewport center. Each copy rotates as a whole, yielding 24 eyes and 12 mouths on one circle from a single camera and face detection. The radius contracts on narrow screens so the copies fit horizontally. The source composition and copies update with the tracked camera frames; no extra camera or WebGL contexts are created. Browser and physical-device appearance and performance remain unverified.
