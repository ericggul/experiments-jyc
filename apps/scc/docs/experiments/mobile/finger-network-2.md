# Mobile finger-network/2

- **Route:** `/mobile/finger-network/2`
- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Baseline:** `/mobile/finger-network/1`'s black field, white complete graph, and touch tracking. The baseline remains independently addressable.

The changed relation is five fingertips becoming the head, two hands, and two feet of one moving 2D human. The highest contact at the moment the fifth finger arrives becomes the head; the next two by height become left and right hands by x-position, and the lowest two become left and right feet. These roles remain bound to their touch identifiers while all five are held, so crossing fingers do not swap limbs. When fewer than two contacts remain, the figure stays (see the persistence section below; until 2026-10-02 it faded and the graph returned). The graphical body interpolates the endpoints with shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, neck, and torso; the rendering adds shaded volumes, facial detail, subtle breathing, and blinking. Since 2026-09-30 the shoulders sit a fixed 66 units (× body scale) below the head center instead of 28% of body height, so the neck no longer stretches. A same-day trial replaced the face with a plain textured oval; it was reverted and the original face restored. It is a stylized sculptural motion graphic, not a camera-derived or anatomically measured body.

The body follows touch positions with a damped response and uses the same bounded 2D canvas and 1.5 DPR cap as `/1`. A generated grayscale skin texture is clipped at low opacity to the shaded face, torso, and limbs; it is loaded only by `/2`. If Safari briefly reports zero contacts, the last binding is kept for 180 ms so a returning finger is not reread. The instruction text shows only until the first figure forms. The iPhone's five-contact limit makes five the full-body state; six or more contacts return the graph.

## Character option (2026-10-02)

A small `옵션` button at the bottom opens two choices above it, `기본` and `일론 머스크`; `닫기` collapses them (the same controls as [`/3`](finger-network-3.md)). `기본` is the default and is the sculptural 2D body described here, unchanged. The instruction and readout move up to clear the controls, and touches that start on the controls are not fingers.

`일론 머스크` replaces the 2D body with a rigged, realistic 3D model of Elon Musk drawn by three.js on a transparent WebGL canvas over the field; the network, contact rings, readout, fade, and the whole rig (fit, freeze, morph) are shared. three.js and the model (`public/assets/finger-network/elon-musk.glb`) load only when the option is first chosen. While loading, or if the file is missing or lacks the expected bones, the 2D body stands in. The model is posed from the 2D pose each frame (`screen/model-figure.ts`): its size follows the body frame (head-to-ankle = 470 rest units), the hips sit on the pelvis, the torso and head turn toward the neck and head, and each arm and leg is a rigid two-bone reach (`model/limb.ts`) toward its hand or foot that bends to the side of the 2D elbow or knee. Because a real body's limbs do not stretch, a hand or foot spread wider than the limb can reach stops short of its finger. The figure faces the viewer, so the screen-left limbs are the model's right limbs.

The model is generated, not scanned. About 150 reference photos were collected from Wikimedia Commons (freely licensed; kept outside the repository with a credits file). Gemini turned the best studio portraits into front, left, and back full-body views on white; their backgrounds were removed locally (rembg), because with the white background the shape model built a flat card. The front view went to the public Hunyuan3D-2.1 Space's shape-only endpoint (the textured endpoint exceeds the free GPU limit); the resulting 316k-face body was then painted by `scripts/texture-finger-network-model.py`, which projects the three views along their axes onto the mesh, keeps only unoccluded vertices that face each view, and bakes the blend into one 2048 px texture on 60k faces. `scripts/rig-finger-network-model.py` rigs it headlessly in Blender: joints from the mesh's own cross-sections, heat weights on a 5 mm voxel proxy transferred back, arm weights removed from torso skin below the shoulder (otherwise the armpit stretched like a web), and WebP textures. The asset is 2.5 MB. Expected bones: `hips spine chest neck head`, and `shoulder upperArm forearm hand thigh shin foot` with `_L`/`_R`. The face's surface detail is shallow; the likeness comes mostly from the projected texture, and the sides, seen only in the generated side view, are less faithful. In a numeric check, reachable hands and feet landed exactly on their targets, and Blender renders of raised-arm and spread-leg poses deformed cleanly apart from a slight web under a fully raised arm. Rendering and frame time on a device are unverified.

## Persistence and morphing (2026-10-02)

Lifting a finger freezes the part it held: the released part stays exactly where it was in the body frame instead of springing back to rest, and the remaining fingers are fitted against that frozen pose, so they carry the shape without changing it (two fingers move, turn, and size it rigidly). Fingers almost never lift together, so without this the shape decayed through the lower counts before the last finger left. Once fewer than two fingers remain, the body keeps its last shape where the fingers left it, still breathing and blinking, and fades out linearly over 3 s (the readout fades with it and shows `n=0`). After that it is cleared and the instruction text returns. (Earlier on 2026-10-02 it stayed indefinitely.) Contact rings vanish with the fingers. When two or more fingers return mid-fade, the body fades back in and the hand is read as usual and the held body morphs into the new fit instead of reappearing; three- and four-finger hands keep the held body's size. After every hand change the follow rate eases from 4/s to the tracking rate of 19/s over 0.6 s, so the change reads as one transformation and dragging stays tight afterwards. Fitting five fingers in the same body frame as two to four is what keeps these transitions continuous.

## Two to four fingers (2026-09-30)

The figure now appears from two contacts, and the number of fingers sets the body's degrees of freedom. A rest-pose body is fitted to the held points by a 2D similarity transform (position, rotation, size), so the fit itself sets how much the body can move:

- **2 fingers:** top is the head, bottom is the point between the feet. The body is rigid, and the two fingers only move, turn (a full 360°), and resize it (0.25–2.4× the rest size). No joint moves.
- **3 fingers:** top is the head, the lower two are the feet. Position and rotation follow the best fit, while size stays at the value it had when the hand changed. The legs bend through the existing pose builder. The free arms are coupled to the stance, so spreading the feet raises the arms like a jumping jack.
- **4 fingers:** the top two are the hands and the bottom two are the feet. The free head rides above the torso and leans toward the hands.
- **5 fingers:** height ranking assigns roles as before. Since 2026-10-02 the five points are also fitted by the similarity transform (with their own fitted size), and every endpoint is placed exactly on its finger in that body frame. Previously the frame was the screen.

Lifting a finger keeps the parts that are still held, so the body hangs from whatever remains; until 2026-10-02 the released limb sprang back to its rest place in the body frame. Adding a finger rereads the whole hand by height. Frame and endpoints follow with the same damped response; rotation takes the shortest turn. Contact rings mark only held parts. The figure is drawn in an upright local frame under a canvas transform, so the renderer's upright shading assumptions hold when the body is rotated.

A small centered monospace readout at the bottom streams the body's state: held-finger count and input DOF, held-part bitmask (head, left hand, right hand, left foot, right foot), pelvis position, lean, size, height, hand span, stance, elbow and knee angles, breath, eye state, and figure opacity. It is written directly to the DOM at about 12 Hz and fades with the figure.

Shadow blur now applies only to silhouette shapes (limbs, torso, head, hair, ears, hands, feet); interior joints, limb highlights, chest and abdominal marks, toes, and facial features draw without it. The glow at the outline is intended to look the same, though this is unmeasured on device, and so is the frame-time gain.

Pure rig tests cover role assignment, identifier persistence through finger crossings, finite joint geometry, exact rigid two-finger placement across rotation, held-limb articulation for three and four fingers, and binding persistence as fingers lift. Rendering and multi-finger motion on an actual iPhone remain unverified, including whether rereading by height on a finger's return feels abrupt.
