# Gaze tracking — /mobile/gaze-tracking/1–2

2026-09-24. `/1` preserves the mobile-first example in which two dots move on the interface according to the left and right iris positions. The front camera's video is processed locally by [MediaPipe Face Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker/web_js) and is not shown or stored. The user explicitly starts camera access; leaving the page or hiding the tab stops the tracks. It also works with a desktop webcam.

The task returns 478 face landmarks, including iris centres. Each iris is measured relative to its own eye corners and eyelids, so head translation has less effect than mapping raw camera coordinates. The horizontal camera image is reversed into the viewer's direction. The dot motion is smoothed and inference is capped at about ten frames per second to limit mobile main-thread work. The task's `.task` model comes from the [official model bundle](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/face_landmarker/face_landmarker/float16/latest/face_landmarker.task) (SHA-256 `64184e229b263107bc2b804c6625db1341ff2bb731874b0bcc2fe6544e0bc9ff`). SIMD/non-SIMD Wasm files are copied from the installed `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.0.1 package. These files are served from this app so the runtime does not depend on a third-party CDN.

The dot renderer interpolates toward each new gaze target on every animation frame. Its time-based damping smooths the visible movement between the slower camera estimates without changing detection or the projected gaze coordinates.

The surface now fills the viewport. Only `카메라 켜기` is visible before camera access; once access is granted the button is removed, and camera access resumes automatically when a hidden tab becomes visible again. The former centre calibration, header, legend, notes, and secondary controls were removed. The local Wasm loaders route the exact TensorFlow Lite `INFO: Created TensorFlow Lite XNNPACK delegate for CPU.` message to `console.info` so Next's development console error interceptor does not present this successful CPU initialization as an error; all other stderr messages still go to `console.error`.

2026-09-25. `/2` uses the same gaze estimates on each existing clone at `/mobile/gaze-tracking/2/1`–`/13`. A bottom-right filter panel opens in liquid mode by default and also offers the difference-circle mode. Circle diameter starts at 100px and ranges from 50–300px. Its white fills invert all three underlying color channels; red and blue outlines distinguish the eyes. The circles are portaled directly to the document body so blending reaches the clone backdrop.

Liquid mode captures the existing clone's visible DOM into a texture and uses a GLSL displacement field around both gaze points to remap those interface pixels. Its panel adjusts radius (80–400px, initially 180px) and strength (0–100, initially 50). Two plain 5px red/blue points mark gaze centers; they are excluded from the interface capture so they appear only once. The left field turns clockwise and the right field counterclockwise in the displayed texture. Their overlap adds a localized pressure and shear term between the two points. It does not add a separate scene or alter clone code. The canvas passes pointer events through to the underlying interface. Captures are coalesced after DOM and scroll changes, limited to 1.6 million pixels and at most about two per second; the shader draws at up to about 30 frames per second. The source at `/face-voronoi/3` informed the pixel-coordinate warp-before-sampling approach, while the actual source texture here is the clone UI. This is a visual deformation of a sampled interface: text, animated media, and hit areas may not exactly match the warped pixels between captures.

The third mode, `시공간`, is an independent gaze-driven adaptation of [Spacetime Maps](https://github.com/vvolhejn/spacetime-maps). Its source connects image texture coordinates to a triangular mesh and draws the grid from the same displaced vertices. The first version changed spring target lengths near each eye, but the two eyes usually landed near the same screen region, where opposing contributions canceled and the 40%-of-cell displacement cap hid what gaze controlled. Each eye now centers a compactly supported rotational displacement field: in displayed screen coordinates the left turns clockwise and the right counterclockwise. For distance `r` from an eye and selected radius `R`, the local angle magnitude is `0.9 × (strength / 50) × (1 - (r/R)^2)^3` when `r < R`, and zero outside. When the two circles overlap, a moving shear across the line between the eyes and a smaller compression along that line create a visible collision band instead of a blank cancellation zone. The curl and collision oscillate modestly over time; reduced-motion preference holds their phase still. The same field bends the clone texture and grid together. Outside both radii, the mesh remains exactly rectangular. The grid has three subdivisions per former interval on each axis, roughly nine times as many cells, with at most 120×120 vertices. Fixed mesh buffers and a WebGL vertex shader perform the warp without per-frame CPU spring simulation; drawing stays capped at about 24 frames per second. Radius (100–450px, initially 230px) and strength (0–100, initially 60) adjust the gaze field; `그리드` toggles the grid. This is a visual distortion of a planar UI, not a claim of intrinsic non-Euclidean curvature: a smooth, invertible deformation of a flat plane preserves its intrinsic flatness. The experiment uses no travel-time data. The same 5px gaze points are shown in this mode. As with liquid mode, visual pixels can differ from the live DOM's hit areas between captures.

Changing `시공간 → 액화` initially left the former grid image on screen, because the two WebGL renderers reused one canvas/context and inherited its state. Each mode now mounts a separate canvas and renderer lifecycle. Chrome confirmed the grid disappears and the liquid warp responds to its strength slider after this transition.

These dots show **relative eye direction**, not a measured screen fixation point. A single front camera cannot recover an accurate per-person screen coordinate; head rotation, glasses, eyelid occlusion, lighting and camera placement can all shift the estimate. Device compatibility and perceptual accuracy still require device testing.
