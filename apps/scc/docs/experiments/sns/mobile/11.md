# Instagram mobile — /sns/mobile/11

2026-09-23. An editable, reference-informed Instagram mobile reconstruction for moving through a dense social feed. The prior nine-post sample stopped after five feed photos and placed Create in the center of the old five-tab bar. This revision uses Home, Reels, Messages, Search and Profile. The current visual pass puts Create at the left of the centered wordmark, Activity at the right, and the five destinations in a floating translucent pill, following Instagram's current US iPhone listing. Post actions now include visible counts and a session-only repost toggle. The 100 photo posts are all reachable by feed scroll and Explore; accounts alternate in the feed. Reels, profile grids, stories, comments, saves, follows, local posting, the share sheet, and post options continue to connect through one local model. Messages now opens a local thread, and sharing a post adds it to the chosen thread. The Reels Friends choice shows the boundary of this preview: there is no real friend activity to populate it.

The initial inventory contains **100 photo posts with 100 different Unsplash source photo IDs and 100 different captions**, plus two bundled video Reels with separate thumbnails. This count excludes new posts that participants add during a session. The photos were selected by subject from public Unsplash search pages and given image-specific descriptions; the account identities, social relations, locations, post text, comments and engagement numbers are fictional. The feed includes personal updates, a shop announcement, conversation prompts, varied caption lengths, and selected comments. Photo IDs are distinct; re-crops or reordered repeats are not counted as new posts. External photo delivery depends on Unsplash.

## Reference boundary

- [Meta's Instagram navigation announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/in-india-instagram-debuts-a-reels-first-experience-for-its-mobile-app/) (28 September 2025) explicitly announces DMs moving to the center of the navigation bar and Reels moving to the second tab, with swiping between tabs. The same note describes Reels-first landing as a **limited India test**. This experiment retains Home as its initial screen; it does not treat the India test as a worldwide default. The announcement establishes intended navigation roles, not a verified 2026 global rollout or exact pixel geometry.
- [Meta's repost and Reels Friends announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/08/new-instagram-features-help-you-connect/) (6 August 2025) establishes Reels/Friends as separate views and reposts. Here the Friends view remains empty because the fictional accounts have no evidenced friend activity. Reposting toggles only the visible local post state; it does not distribute a post to another account's profile.
- [Meta's 2026 Instagram brand update](https://www.meta.com/design-at-meta/blog/the-new-instagram-brand-identity/) (13 August 2026) announces a new wordmark rolling out globally. The header uses the corresponding [2026 vector wordmark](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Instagram_wordmark_(2026).svg) rather than a generic script font; the App Store promotional screenshots still depict the prior script, so brand rollout and screenshot UI are distinct pieces of evidence.

Source mode: reference-informed editable reconstruction. The App Store Home frame supplies a current 430 px-wide iPhone composition but does not expose CSS, device scale, account flags, or all interaction states. This pass follows its visible header order, post action grammar, feed density, and floating nav, while using the separately announced 2026 wordmark. Icon drawings, account content, generated engagement counts, local activity, message handling and secondary sheets are experiment adaptations. The share, repost and message actions stay inside this mounted session. Posting uses eight sample images; it does not upload a file or publish to Instagram.

The 2026-09-23 icon correction uses the visible App Store Home frame for the outlined heart, rounded comment bubble, two-arrow repost, paper-plane send, bookmark, three-dot menu, and bottom navigation shapes. Post-action icons are 25–26 px. Their groups flow according to the width of each count, while Save stays at the right edge. These are hand-drawn SVG approximations of that one visible promotional state; other Instagram variants remain outside this reference boundary.

After user review, the route was checked in Chrome at its live HTTPS URL. That check showed that the first correction's clapper stripes, up-right paper plane, and solid Home icon diverged from the App Store frame. They were replaced with the reference's plain rounded Reels square, downward plane, and Home doorway. The first post's Like, Comments, Save, and Repost controls were exercised in Chrome; toggles were returned to their initial state. A further Chrome pass corrected the action-row height from 47 to 60 px and adjusted icon and count sizes. It initially copied absolute horizontal positions from the App Store raster, but that frame has much longer counts than this fictional feed. The fixed grid made empty gaps between short counts; a subsequent Chrome review replaced it with flowing action groups and a right-aligned Save. This is a comparison to one promotional screenshot, not proof of parity across Instagram devices or accounts.

Their glyphs now come from Instagram's own icon source, not from drawings. Geometry is in
[`igds-glyphs.ts`](../../../../components/mobile/clone/11/screen/igds-glyphs.ts).

**A failed first pass, kept on record.** An earlier pass the same day used paths recalled from memory as "instagram.com icons". They were the old **Pano** family. Their share plane is sharp, while the 2026 app uses the rounded **Prism** plane. Their liked heart was also a different path. The user caught the error. Do not write platform glyphs from memory.

### Fit against the 2026 feed frame

Each source path was rasterised and fitted by scale and offset against the dark-pixel mask of the US feed frame's action row. The phone screen spans 917 px, so a 390 pt iPhone gives 2.351 px/pt.

| Action | Source glyph | IoU | Fitted box |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Like | HeartPanoOutline | 0.942 | 24.1 pt |
| Comment | CommentPanoOutline | 0.940 | 24.0 pt |
| Repost | ResharePanoOutline | 0.952 | 24.0 pt |
| Share | DirectPrismOutline24 | 0.912 | 23.8 pt |
| Share (old Pano polygon, rejected) | SharePosts polygon | 0.477 | — |
| Save | SavePanoOutline | 0.881 | 23.8 pt |

The remaining differences are anti-aliasing at about 1 px. Save scores lowest: the app's two lower points are slightly sharper and its sides slightly thinner, by less than 0.5 pt. No other save glyph is served. On the Reels rail, the bright video background makes the mask unreliable, but Prism share (0.68) still beats Pano share (0.36).

The CSS now uses 24 px icons, a 6 px icon–count gap, an 18 px group gap, a 12/11 px inset and a 14 px icon top. All per-icon `transform` scales are gone. The count format follows Instagram's short summary: `749`, `2.7K`, `36.2K`, `823K`, `1.2M`. The reposted state shows the check-repost glyph. The active nav tab uses the filled Prism glyph, matching the Reels frame, where the active Reels icon is filled. The nav stroke is the source value, 2.

Boundary: the glyph files are Instagram's web IGDS assets. The iOS app was checked only through rasterised official promotional frames, not a device capture. No browser check of this route was run for this pass. Its rendered row should still be compared against the feed frame above.

Static verification on 2026-09-23: 100 photo entries, 100 unique source IDs, 100 unique captions; scoped ESLint, SCC typecheck, and diff check passed.
