# TikTok mobile — /sns/mobile/12

Experimental, source-informed mobile interface, 2026-09-23. Its distinct relation is the passage between a full-screen video feed and the smaller contexts around a video: comments, creator profile, search, saved/liked grids and activity. The preceding SNS mobile variants provide the independent folder and local-state baseline; their visual systems are not reused.

The feed starts with five existing local portrait MP4s and poster frames generated from those exact files. It then requests 30 **distinct, ordered, curated** portrait WebM candidates from Wikimedia Commons in groups of five. A direct Commons metadata audit found 29 eligible remote files under the WebM, portrait and 35 MB rules; `File:Ikusgela-Patata.webm` exceeds the size limit. Thus **34 distinct source videos is the present maximum** (five local plus 29 remote), plus any locally posted video. The remote titles were selected by subject, duration, portrait shape and file size from the [Vertical videos category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Vertical_videos) and three individual NASA Commons pages; exact titles are recorded in `model/commons.ts`. The browser resolves each selected file through the [Commons Action API](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/API:Imageinfo), reads the artist and license metadata, and displays a link to the original file and license from its local creator profile. Remote video availability, codec support, metadata, and network access remain dependencies. Files missing metadata or failing the checks are skipped, so a visit can contain fewer than 34 playable posts.

For You and Following have separate clip sets; following a creator affects the latter. Vertical swipe, wheel, Arrow and Page keys move to the next or previous item **without wrapping**. Near the end of loaded For You posts, the next group is requested; reaching the boundary while loading queues one forward step. The screen shows loading, retryable request failure, unavailable-video and finite-end states. The mechanics are discrete video changes driven by gestures, rather than native scroll snapping. Video tap/keyboard, sound and seek controls affect playback. Likes, favorites, comments, comment likes, follows and local posts update this visit's state. Creator and own profiles expose video grids; a grid tile opens video detail and Back returns to the originating page. Search filters loaded videos and accounts, and Inbox reflects actions made in the preview. A new post uses a local video file and never publishes remotely. The five starter creators, captions, counts and comments are invented for this trial; the Commons posts use credited artist metadata and descriptive trial captions, with local counts starting at zero. None is attributed to a real TikTok account.

## Reference boundary

- [TikTok 2025 trend report](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Whats_Next_2025_Trend_Report.pdf) shows a TikTok-authored mobile video frame with Following / For You at top, the right action rail, caption/sound at the foot and bottom navigation. This was the main feed visual reference; it is a promotional still, not a measured current app capture.
- [TikTok Help: recommendation surfaces](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content) distinguishes For You, Following and Search. This supports their separate destinations, not any claim that the local ordering reproduces TikTok's ranking.
- [TikTok Help: liking](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/liking) documents side-button/double-tap likes and liked videos under Profile; [TikTok Help: comments](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/messaging-and-notifications/comments) documents entering comments from a video's side action.
- [GSMA's mobile TikTok guide, pp. 25–27](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/MISTT-TikTok-InDepth-English.pdf) describes feed swipe and the side action order. Public [profile-grid](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michael-isaac-2b5076211_ive-started-my-tiktok-journey-at-the-start-activity-7282050543336722432-GY0Y) and [search-result](https://x.com/i/communities/1993724809553842548) screenshots informed three-column video tiles and search composition. These frames differ in date, account and region; no single source establishes pixel parity across all screens.

Inner pages are semantic reconstructions from the cited public material and local product decisions. Search, Friends and Inbox are local sample-data flows; there is no sign-in, recommendation engine, messaging, remote sharing service, upload or persistence. Creators remain fictional (Unsplash photos stand in for their avatars; Commons artists use their own video frame), so no real creator is impersonated. The finite Commons selection does not reproduce TikTok's continually replenished personalized inventory.

**Remaining differences:**
- No LIVE, Explore or Shop destinations, and no real messaging.
- Create opens a local "Post" form, not the camera shown in the App Store frame. The browser has no camera pipeline here.
- Friends is a list and grid, not a friends' video feed.
- There's no explicit mute control, because the device volume does that job in the real app.
- When the browser blocks autoplay, playback falls back to muted.
- The neighbouring page shows its poster during a drag, not live video.
- Region and A/B variants of TikTok differ, so the frames above set the target.
