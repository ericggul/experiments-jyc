# Traffic light 2

`/transportation/traffic-light/2`, 2026-10-06: an independent copy of
[`/1`](README.md) with the same pole repeated fifty times in a straight row
down one road (no road drawn). The question is what one ordinary object
becomes when repeated at street scale: a receding rhythm of arms and lamps.

Changed variable: pole count (1 → 50) along -z at a chosen spacing. Invariants:
every pole is `/1`'s model and signal options; all poles switch together on
the default plan.

- `간격`: a 1–50 m slider in 1 m steps (default 5 m); the row is 0.05–2.5 km
  long. Below about 1.6 m footings, and below about 1 m visors and back heads,
  interpenetrate; the poles are not separated or clipped. Option rows can be choices or ranges (`screen/options.tsx`); the slider
  keeps the panel's plain white type, with a thin track and the value in metres
  beside it. Spacing alone does not rebuild anything: every instance keeps its
  pole index and pole-frame matrix, and `place` rewrites instance matrices
  (0.3 ms headless for the largest configuration, against ~74 ms to rebuild).
- The opening view stands on the kerb behind the first pole and looks down the
  row; fog runs from 60 m to 85 % of the row length, so the far poles dissolve
  into the horizon. Sky dome and ground reach 4 km; the dome is drawn first
  without depth so poles beyond it still show.
- The sun's shadow box (±30 m) sits just ahead of the camera and moves with
  every orbit; poles outside it cast no shadow. One box covering the whole row
  would leave shadows blurred beyond use.

Since 2026-10-06 the page opens on the user's chosen setup (`OPENING` in

## Arrangements and variations

2026-10-06. Where poles stand and how they differ is data in
`model/arrangement.ts`, not interface code:

- A pole is a `PoleSpec`: base position (x, z), heading, and `rise` (metres
  added to the shaft below the arm).
- `LAYOUTS` place the poles and set their number:
  - `직선`: 50 poles down -z at `간격`.
  - `원형`: one pole stays where it stands and 50 arms fan out around it,
    evenly over `펼침 각도` (30–360°; a partial fan spans both ends). The poles
    share one trunk: only the first draws base and shaft, the shaft stretched
    to the highest arm; the others draw only their tops. Spacing is hidden.
    First version, rejected the same day: 50 poles stood on a circle of
    circumference 50 × spacing with arms facing in, out or along it; the
    intended form was arms around one fixed pole.
  - `격자`: `행` × `열` poles (1–20 each, default 10 × 10), `간격` apart both
    ways, centred across, all facing the viewer. Arms longer than the spacing
    reach past the neighbouring pole.
- `VARIATIONS` then alter them in order. `높이`: each pole's rise is drawn
  independently, `높이 분포` 같음 / 정규 (σ = 편차) / 균등 (±편차) / 로그정규
  (right-skewed, scale = 편차), `높이 편차` 0–6 m. Rises are clamped at −1 m,
  so the arm heads stay above 4 m and clear of the pole head; wide normal
  spreads pile some poles at that floor.
- Each entry declares its parameters, and the option panel is generated from
  them in three sections, set apart by space: signal, layout (only the chosen
  layout's parameters show), variations. Stochastic variations get `다시 섞기`,
  which advances one seed; each variation draws from its own stream, so the
  same seed always gives the same arrangement.
- Adding an experiment is adding a registry entry: a layout's `place`, or a
  variation's `apply`, with its parameters.

A change of pole number (another layout, or the grid's size) rebuilds the
instance buffers once; any other arrangement change only moves instances.
Headless, one three-colour head per pole: the circle drew one footing of 50
with its lamps on a 5.8 m ring round the pole; the grid built 100 poles
(1.8 M vertices) and 400 at 20 × 20 (7.2 M vertices), 14 draw calls each.
Larger heads multiply the vertex count; the 20 × 20 limit is unmeasured on
devices.

Rendering stays instanced. The pole is split into base (footing, plate, bolts,
gussets, hand hole, pole head), shaft and top (cap, arm joint, arm, arm heads).
Each part is merged and instanced separately: the base stands at the pole's
spot, the shaft stretches about its foot, the top rises. Heads keep their
shape. Any arrangement change only rewrites instance matrices. Headless,
circle with normal heights, 2 four-colour heads plus pole head: 18 draw
calls, 80 ms to build, 0.4 ms to reshuffle; arm lamps sat at exactly
5.2 m + rise, pole-head lamps and footings unmoved.

The `신호 주기` option was removed here; every pole runs the default plan.

## Signal timing

`신호 동기` (a variation, so it has `다시 섞기`): `맞춤` runs every pole on one
clock, as before; `어긋남` keeps the plan's cycle and speed for every pole and
gives each its own start point, drawn uniformly over the cycle. (Asked first
with differing cycle lengths averaging the plan's; revised the same day to
one shared cycle.) The clock lives in the canvas, not React state: each pole's
local time is elapsed × rate + phase × cycle (`PoleSpec.rate` stays 1 and is
kept for later trials), the timer wakes at the soonest change on any pole, and
updates batch at 30 Hz.

## Lamp faces

`램프` (`model/lamp-faces.ts`): `LED` is the emitter board; `트럼프·오바마·바이든`
puts Trump, Obama and Biden on the red, amber and green lamps. The goal, as
the user set it: each must read as that person at a glance, even far away.

What works (browser-checked 2026-10-06): the portrait in its own continuous
tones, tinted by the lamp's colour. The picture is cropped to its mask's
bounding square so the head fills the lens; outside the mask ellipse the board
is dark, with a short feathered edge; luminance inside is stretched between
its 2nd and 98th percentiles (gamma 1.15). Faces run at 0.3 of the board's
emissive intensity (`FACE_INTENSITY`) so they keep their tones instead of
blooming to white. All three read as the person on the nearest several poles.

Failed the same day, each judged worse by the user:
1. Continuous brightness over the whole square photo: a vague tinted picture,
   background included.
2. Masked, three-level emitters on a fine grid: crisper but read as a halftone;
   red bloomed pink.
3. Variant of 2 at 0.45 intensity with an unsharp boost and a one-third middle
   level.
4. Bold binary emitters at LED pitch, like the arrow: strong, but the people
   were no longer recognisable.
Quantising a face into emitters destroys the tonal cues that identify it;
recognition needed the continuous tones.

Faces load once per choice and are cached; switching swaps material textures
and the face intensity. A new texture trial is a new `LAMP_FACES` entry.

## Performance

One pole is built in its own frame and repeated by instancing. Its steel and
clamps are merged per material and instanced once per pole; housings, boards
and lenses are instanced across all poles. Merged geometry is indexed
(`mergeVertices`), and curve segments on visors, door holes, gaskets and clamp
bands are lowered from `/1`'s.

Headless build, 50 poles:

| Configuration | Draw calls | Instances | Vertices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 head, 40 m | 11 | 550 | 0.91 M (3.63 M before indexing) |
| 3 four-colour heads + back + pole head, 20 m | 14 | 2,450 | 4.01 M (15.9 M before) |

Draw calls do not grow with pole or head count. The scene renders only on
signal change, orbit, option change or resize; orbiting also re-renders the
shadow pass. There is no per-instance distance culling or level of detail.

Unverified: not browser-checked; appearance at 1–5 km, fog balance and frame
time on devices are unknown. Lint, TypeScript and the navigation test pass.

Open question: a green wave (연동), offsetting each pole's plan by distance
over progression speed, would make the row's timing a relation rather than a
unison.
