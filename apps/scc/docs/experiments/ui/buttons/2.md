# UI Buttons 2 — LinkedIn connection field

- **Route:** `/ui/buttons/2`
- **Date:** 2026-09-04
- **Baseline:** LinkedIn's local People You May Know `Connect` action in
  `components/ui/sns/linkedin/1`, together with LinkedIn's documented invitation
  and first-degree connection relation.

## Trial contract

1. **Participant situation:** a viewer meets the professional network request
   as a field rather than beside individual profiles.
2. **Primary parameter:** each record's relationship progression:
   `Connect → Pending → Connected`.
3. **Perceptual job:** preserve the compact outlined LinkedIn action grammar
   while a mouse sweep makes request, waiting, and relation accumulate across
   the page.
4. **Interaction job:** mouse entry advances one local record one state. The
   state cycle is a bounded visual model, not a claim that LinkedIn exposes
   these exact three labels as one control in every product context.
5. **Wrapper justification:** the white LinkedIn action on its pale neutral
   canvas remains recognisable; the only alteration is its repeated scale and
   relation state.
6. **Removal test:** no profile, count, suggestion card, title, or feed is
   added. The changing relationship button field is the whole visible work.

## Palette trial

- The bottom selector switches between `3` and `10` colours without changing a
  record's three relationship states or its place in the grid.
- `3` preserves the restrained LinkedIn relation grammar: blue outline, grey
  pending, blue filled connection.
- `10` assigns every record one stable colour from a muted web-action palette.
  Alternating records begin as outlined and solid actions; their subsequent
  pending and connected states move through a tint, outline, or deeper fill.
  This is a chromatic redistribution of the button grammar, not a claim about
  LinkedIn's product palette.

## Pointillist image trial

- The bottom control retains `3` and `10` relationship-colour modes, then adds
  three image sources: `Mona Lisa`, `Great Wave`, and `Earthrise`. Selecting a
  thumbnail makes the repeated buttons form the selected image.
- The new variable is not a bitmap layer behind the controls. A client-side
  canvas samples the source at the actual column and row count of the button
  grid, so every visible button receives one sampled colour.
- Cover cropping uses the physical button grid aspect ratio (`columns × 36` by
  `rows × 13`), not the logical sample-count ratio. Source images remain at
  their native aspect ratio and crop from the centre when necessary; they are
  never stretched to fit the field.
- Each source has a deliberately chosen ten-colour palette suited to its own
  value structure: ochre, umber, olive, and skin lights for the portrait;
  indigo, blue, cream, and grey for the wave; black, lunar grey, and earth blue
  for Earthrise. Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion distributes the residual into
  neighbouring buttons, so colour mixing occurs as a field of points rather
  than flat posterised regions.
- The image does not replace the button grammar. Every point retains its
  `Connect`, `Pending`, or `Connected` label, 0.5px border, rounded form, and
  outline/solid treatment. The sampled source colour becomes the button tone:
  brighter points favour outlines, while darker and middle-value points favour
  fills. Hovering advances the same three local states through an outline,
  tint, or deeper fill.

## Invariants

- The field meets and overfills the viewport edge. Each control is 36 × 13px
  with no inter-button margin, yielding a dense repeated field.
- Grid capacity is measured from its rendered button geometry and recalculated
  through one animation-frame-batched `ResizeObserver` path.
- The visible field is one Canvas 2D surface, not thousands of DOM buttons.
  Its state is a compact `Uint8Array`; pointer movement calculates one grid
  index and redraws only that cell. Canvas remains labelled as one interactive
  button field for assistive technology.
- Full-field redraw occurs only after a grid, palette, or image change. Canvas
  backing resolution is capped at twelve million pixels while preserving up to
  a 2× device scale, preventing high-DPI displays from allocating an
  unbounded bitmap.
- Image sampling runs only after a source image has loaded and only when its
  source or the grid dimensions change. Sampling is scheduled in one animation
  frame; hover does not enter React rendering.

## Reference note

LinkedIn documents a pending invitation becoming a first-degree connection on
acceptance; it also treats following as a separate relation, so `Follow` is not
inserted into this trial's three-state cycle. [LinkedIn invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a542708/invitations-on-linkedin-overview?lang=en) · [Follow and connect](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6266298)
